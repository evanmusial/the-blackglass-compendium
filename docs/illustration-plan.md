# Illustration and Diagram Plan

## Purpose and scope

This is the production backlog for high-impact art accompanying the current
Compendium: astronomical portraits, orbital atlases, inhabited places, individual
technologies, historical scenes, explanatory diagrams, and abstract evidence.
It is an art plan, not an adoption of new geography, apparatus, incidents, or
canon. All new assets below are **planned**. The existing Kelan–Ruva infographic
is recorded separately near the end.

The strongest images should make the reader understand a physical relationship,
recognize the civilization that depends on it, or feel the cost of its failure.
Living cities, work, medical care, and recovery need substantial space alongside
ruins and weapons. Each forbidden system should have its own visual language.

The catalogue contains **80 asset briefs**. A brief can produce a reusable body
render, an inset, a sequence, or a full spread; it does not necessarily require
one printed page. Some briefs are intended to combine into one plate.

## Priority, medium, and readiness

**P1** identifies essential explanatory art or a signature image. **P2** develops
the world and its consequences. **P3** is an optional expansion or a subject whose
production should follow additional design work.

**PR** means a photoreal astronomical render, suitable for the user's planet,
moon, and star workflow. **Scene** means an illustrated environment, person,
artifact, or historical reconstruction. **Diagram** means a constructed vector
or technical graphic, potentially incorporating rendered bodies. **Abstract**
means a deliberately nonliteral visualization. **Archive** means typography,
material evidence, marginalia, or a documentary facsimile.

**Model** means that the relevant numerical relationships are supplied by current
canon. **Interpretive** means the subject is supported but its visible design
must be presented as an artist's reconstruction. **Reserved** means concept art
for the developing Unhidden or Manifold; drawing it does not adopt its proposed
history. **Design needed** identifies a specific missing world decision that
must precede an authoritative map or portrait. A model can still be provisional.

Captions should identify a schematic, historical reconstruction, documented
practice, disputed account, or concept reconstruction where appropriate. A
polished image must not look like additional forensic proof. Exact layouts,
surface patterns, faces, insignia, and machinery interiors remain illustrative
unless the text actually fixes them.

## Orbital diagrams and system atlases

**Kelan–Ruva is currently the only star system with a quantitative system-level orbital model.** Its central
binary and the Kharosi moon subsystem support additional quantitative diagrams.
Valec, Issa, and Apsis can have contextual illustrations now, but their complete
system maps need more design. The plan explicitly reserves those future maps.

Every orbital graphic should carry **“Orbits and body sizes not to scale”** and
**“Illustrative orbital phase; not a dated ephemeris.”** Keep correct orbital
order and labelled numerical distances even where visual spacing is compressed.
Use separate nested panels for star, planet, moon, and distant-reservoir scales;
an unexplained change of radial scale must not resemble a physical gap.

## Suggested production sequence

Begin with **AST-01–10** as reusable photoreal masters and **ORB-01–03** as the
first orbital diagrams. These establish the home system's size hierarchy,
lighting, labels, and visual consistency before those bodies appear behind
cities or in weapon histories.

Then make one signature explanatory plate for each technology: **RZE-02,
CLN-01, AGR-01, UNH-02, MAN-02, and REH-02**. Pair them with the strongest
human or physical scene for that subject: **RZE-01 or RZE-05, CLN-02, AGR-03,
UNH-01 or UNH-05, MAN-01 or MAN-05, and REH-01**. The Unhidden and Manifold work remains
visibly identified as concept reconstruction.

Add **CIV-01–04, TEC-01–02, and ARC-01–02** early enough that the book's visual
identity includes a home, work, travel, and documentary evidence. Subsequent
passes can expand the named cities, recovery scenes, outer-system activity, and
historical images. ORB-09–11 remain in the future design queue.

This order is a production recommendation, not a fixed illustration count or
page allocation. A body render may appear many times, and a paired mechanism
and scene may share one spread. CIV-04, AST-11–12, and RZE-03 should be planned
together; likewise CIV-08 with AGR-02 and CIV-09 with CLN-01–03.

## Orbital and visual consistency requirements

- Mark barycenters and distinguish a star's barycentric orbit from the binary's
  relative separation. Preserve the six-planet order in every reuse.
- Label distances numerically when orbital spacing is compressed. Keep physical
  diameter comparisons separate from the orbital diagram's enlarged portraits.
- Label moon distances as center-to-center. Show a chosen phase rather than
  implying simultaneous eclipses, fixed moon positions, or a dated observation.
- Keep orbital periods, solar days, and civil calendars distinct. Use the
  documented standard day/year units in technical captions.
- Select one consistent lighting solution per scene. Kelan and Ruva are unequal;
  Kharos is not equally visible from both hemispheres of either tidally locked
  moon. Large close-up insets need their own scale rather than masquerading as
  the actual sky.
- Architecture needs visible support, pressure, access, heat rejection, and
  maintenance consistent with its environment. Ordinary ships need propulsion
  and momentum accounts; an orbit line is not a traffic lane or beam.
- Keep mechanism-specific graphic conventions distinct. Rzez load relations,
  Unhidden anatomy, Manifold charts, Closed Night radiation, Agreement control,
  and Rehearsal particle/quench models should not share one glowing symbol for
  “magic.” Abstract overlays must identify what they represent.
- Make labels, evidence status, and necessary limits legible in the caption.
  Avoid using fine print to rescue a visually false central image.

## Print and asset preparation

For the contemplated trim sizes, these are useful **300-pixel-per-inch** raster
targets for full-page art. They are planning dimensions; the eventual printer
supplies the final bleed and binding template.

| Trim size | Full page at trim | With 0.125-inch bleed on all four edges |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 7 × 10 inches | 2,100 × 3,000 px | 2,175 × 3,075 px |
| 8.5 × 11 inches | 2,550 × 3,300 px | 2,625 × 3,375 px |

Two-page art at trim is approximately **4,200 × 3,000 px** for the smaller
format or **5,100 × 3,300 px** for the larger. Keep faces, labels, specimen
boundaries, and crucial diagram junctions away from the gutter. Set vector text
and linework at final print size; test diagrams in grayscale as well as color.
Fine orbital lines must remain visible on an actual printed proof.

For body renders, retain lossless masters, clean background or alpha versions
where the rendering method supports them, consistent named orientations, and
the chosen lighting notes. Add editorial labels during composition rather than
asking the planet renderer to bake them into the image. Scientific diagrams
should have editable vector masters and explicit numerical inputs.

Each finished asset should record its ID, title, source files and headings,
caption, epoch or interpretive status, dimensions, creator/tool, rights and
reference provenance, and the decisions it visualizes. Save shared art under
`assets/shared/` and weapon art under its entry's asset directory. Developing
concept art should retain its concept slug rather than receiving an invented
permanent weapon ID.

## Decisions to keep visible in the backlog

The main outstanding design work is concentrated in complete Valec, Issa, and
Apsis system maps; named contemporary city maps; repeatable global surface
geography; unassigned clothing and insignia; exact physical forms of incompletely
reconstructed instruments; and concept-local Manifold/Unhidden appearances.
Incidental scene design can remain clearly interpretive. A recurring named place,
quantified orbit, or asserted recovered view needs a consistent written decision.

Portraits of individual named historical people are deliberately secondary to
work, institutions, artifacts, and events. If added later, they should be
identified as reconstructions unless a physical appearance is actually recorded.
The same principle protects the strongest mysteries: art can show what observers
encountered without revealing the Prior Hands, the Narrow Proof, the Manifold's
purpose, or a confirmed catalytic escape from Apsis.
