# Compendium Chronology

## Calendar policy

The Rzez gives a reliable relative anchor but no universal absolute calendar.
The destruction of Valec is **Year Zero of the Compact Calendar**, and the Sixth
Compact follows from that event.

Until another entry establishes broader usage, Compact-relative dates should not
be assumed universal outside the Rzez's historical sphere. Under the provisional
setting scope, the calendar is widely useful to the Codex's compilers and Compact
core but not automatically native to every system or culture.

Kharos's 299-day orbital period is an astronomical fact. It is not yet
established as the length of a Compact Calendar year. Do not convert orbital
periods into Compact-relative dates until the calendar itself is designed.

Humanity's terrestrial origin does not supply an absolute Earth date. Historical
work distinguishes three questions that older popular accounts often collapse:

1. **Physical interval:** how much proper or coordinate time separates two
   events, within stated uncertainty.
2. **Astronomical epoch:** what the Solar or Kelan–Ruva sky looked like in a
   declared reference frame and time scale.
3. **Civil date:** what a society called and numbered that interval under a
   particular calendar and era.

The first two are substantially recoverable for parts of the Founding Interval.
The third is not. Surviving Earth-calendar fragments do not provide a secure
bridge among a numbered civil year, its reference epoch and time standard, and
the clocks and trajectories of the carrier assemblages. A proposed conversion
may be discussed as an in-world scholarly model, but no familiar BCE, CE, or
other numbered Earth year may be presented as authorial truth. The setting has
no concealed canonical Earth date waiting behind the uncertainty.

## Developing chronological constraints

The Unhidden and The Manifold do not yet have named, dated anchor incidents.
Their current sequence belongs here as an authorial constraint rather than as
false precision in the chronological table.

### The Unhidden

1. Human regenerative medicine develops vascularization-independent
   total-boundary scaffolds.
2. The first open wards operate on Kharos during the late Meridian or
   post-Meridian span; the side of Valec on which they fall remains open.
3. Clinical systems acquire sufficient autonomy for consciousness to become
   recognizable; whether one first mantle defines that transition remains
   reserved.
4. One or more offworld states convert the lineage into an open-form combat
   organism and conduct the first wet-cradle deployment.
5. Recovery and humane-custody capacity develops on Avara, while personhood
   proceedings bring the legal consequences back to Kharos.

Exact dates, named facilities, governments, deployments, and judgments remain in
the developing concept until its entry chooses an anchor.

### The Manifold

1. Early accounts predating modern instrumentation associate impossible places
   or intervals with an extrinsic presence. Some records may be securely dated
   even where their interpretation remains weak.
2. A later frontier encounter produces separated instrumental records sufficient
   to distinguish a Manifold rupture from ordinary route failure or folklore.
3. Contemporary multisensor evidence confirms an associated extrinsic presence
   without establishing authorship, motive, or strategy.
4. Kharos, Avara, and Odran develop their present concordance practice in response
   to evidence from outside the home system.

The eventual entry needs at least one exterior Compact-relative anchor. Events
inside a rupture may remain impossible to order even when incidence and
quarantine are dated from unaffected clocks.

## Known period labels

### Founding Interval

The Founding Interval begins with the recoverable uncrewed survey sequence and
ends by convention once Kharos, Avara, and Odran support self-sustaining,
locally born institutions no longer governed as extensions of carrier missions.
It is an interval, not a landing day, dynasty, or single expedition.

Its opening lies several millennia before the earliest securely ordered Meridian
events. Archaeology can order many layers and physical methods can estimate
elapsed durations, but the interval's boundaries vary by discipline. Avaran
histories often close it later than Kharosi histories because continued
atmospheric and ecological stewardship makes “arrival” an enduring civic
condition rather than a completed conquest.

The period must not receive a numbered Earth date. Historians may publish
candidate crosswalks, and recovered sky states may be rendered with great
precision, but the conventional names attached to those states on Earth remain
fragmentary. “Origin Sky” is the normal collective term for the surviving Solar
and departure-era astronomical records; it is evidence, not a functioning
calendar.

### Pre-Meridian

Used for events or artifacts after or outside the Founding Interval that predate
the Meridian Age. Its duration, calendar, and political meaning remain
undefined. Older sources sometimes use *pre-Meridian* loosely enough to include
the founding migrations; precise modern work does not.

### Meridian Age

The broad era spanning the rise of the Quarry Houses, industrial and military
development of Rzez technology, and the destruction of Valec. The draft uses
early, middle, and late subdivisions without exact dates.

The provisional setting scope treats it as a trans-system historical and
technological era, not yet as one universally governed empire.

### Kharosi reconstruction period

A later scholarly and legal context associated with Olan Vey's attributed
commentary. Its exact relationship to the Compact Calendar remains unresolved.

### Post-Compact or current era

The period after Valec and the Sixth Compact, characterized by prohibition,
forensics, defensive architecture, political false alarms, survivor communities,
and rumors of surviving instruments. The Closed Night and Agreement histories
show that the post-Compact period also creates new forbidden systems and new
legal regimes rather than living only in the aftermath of Meridian weapons.

## Chronology questions to preserve

- How wide is the best astronomical date band for each carrier assemblage, and
  which uncertainties arise from stellar models rather than archive loss?
- Which surviving Earth calendar names refer to the same civil system, and
  which only resemble one another after translation?
- Were the carrier assemblages one convoy, distinct direct migrations, or a
  mixture including lost intermediate settlements?
- When did inhabited routes first extend beyond Kelan–Ruva, and which current
  systems preserve genuinely pre-Meridian occupation?

- Does the Sixth Compact begin exactly at Valec's destruction or after a later
  negotiation period while retaining Valec as calendar Year Zero?
- Does “late Meridian Age” end at Valec, at the Compact, or at another event?
- Is the Kharosi reconstruction period contemporaneous with Olan Vey or a later
  period that attributes commentary to him?
- Does the current era have a formal name outside Rzez scholarship?
