# Common Matter Interdict

## Origin and protected interest

The Common Matter Interdict is the post-Apsis legal framework governing
near-Planck convergence arrays, uncharacterized exotic baryonic matter, and
experiments capable of initiating catalytic phase conversion. Its initial text
is adopted in Compact Year +274, three years after the
[Apsis Discharge](../events/apsis-discharge.md).

In the Interdict, **common matter** means the ordinary baryonic material upon
which inhabited environments, bodies, machines, and accessible stars depend.
The phrase does not claim that ordinary nuclei are the universe's absolute
ground state. It treats their continued existence as a shared environmental
condition that no laboratory, state, owner, or consenting test crew may put at
unbounded external risk.

The law is preventive. It does not assume that a catalytic strangelet has ever
been observed. Its premise is narrower: when an experiment's plausible adverse
outcome cannot be recalled, contained after establishment, or limited to those
who authorized it, uncertainty is not evidence of acceptable risk.

## Prohibited acts

The initial Interdict separates ordinary high-energy research from the
architecture that made The Rehearsal singular. It prohibits:

- assembling or synchronizing a convergence crown capable of the prohibited
  spectral-equivalent regime;
- combining such a crown with a baryon-bias pulse or prepared target mass;
- producing or executing an open-quench firing solution at proscribed yield;
- aiming a qualifying array at a planet, star, neutron-degenerate reservoir, or
  inhabited matter stream;
- possessing, transporting, selling, or returning from a range any
  uncharacterized fragment with a strange-rich and anomalously low
  charge-to-mass signature;
- concealing a candidate exotic-matter track, failed sample inventory, or
  unplanned baryon-rich quench; and
- entering, salvaging, reenergizing, or mapping the inner
  [Apsis Range](../places/apsis-range.md) outside an authorized remote-observation
  mandate.

Separately lawful components can become prohibited when their geometry, timing,
power, target package, and control records demonstrate the capacity for one
integrated firing. The Interdict therefore evaluates architecture as well as
ownership. A state may not distribute the crown among nominally independent
contractors and claim that no one possesses a weapon.

## Permitted work

The Interdict is not a general ban on particle accelerators, fusion research,
compact-object astronomy, high-field physics, or strange-matter theory. It
permits, under local law:

- subcritical and deliberately misfocused collision experiments;
- astrophysical observation and passive cosmic-particle detection;
- simulations and mathematical work;
- detector and remote-quarantine engineering;
- power or timing components physically incapable of joining a prohibited
  crown; and
- study of surviving Apsis telemetry that does not disclose a reconstructable
  firing solution.

This boundary remains contested. Some signatories impose broader controls on
timing and projector research. Others argue that the Interdict's architectural
test gives authorities too much discretion over ordinary infrastructure.

## Candidate-fragment custody

The controlling custody rule is **do not turn an observation into a shipment**.
A suspected strangelet is examined remotely, in place, by instruments with
independent acquisition and shutdown paths. No laboratory may improve its
measurement by carrying the candidate toward an inhabited facility or dense
reservoir.

The Interdict does not prescribe one universal disposal method. A fragment's
charge, mass, lifetime, velocity, and interaction cross-section may be precisely
the unknowns that make moving or striking it dangerous. Authorities must record
what they cannot establish rather than translating uncertainty into a safe-
handling fiction.

## Belief, speech, and the Lower State

The Interdict regulates conduct, custody, and weapon architecture. It does not
prohibit belief that strange matter is a lower state, possession of inert
telemetry, worship of The Rehearsal, or the prayer and assemblies of the
[Houses of the Lower State](houses-of-the-lower-state.md).

Waiting Houses may therefore be lawful even where they are feared. The Open
Yield schism becomes criminal when its members acquire projector hardware,
seek candidate fragments, trespass at Apsis, or prepare a dense target. Their
eschatology alone is not treated as an active weapon.

This distinction is both ethical and forensic. Shared religious language is not
an Agreement carrier, and social unanimity does not prove neurocybernetic
coupling. Treating every worshipper as physically contaminated would create
false evidence while driving actual procurement activity out of view.

## Separate jurisdiction

The Common Matter Interdict is independent of the Sixth Compact of Kharos, the
Articles of Open Radiance, the Articles of Bodily Dissent, created-person law,
and Manifold quarantine. Signatories may share inspectors, courts, isolation
practice, or chain-of-custody methods without merging the underlying offenses.

Kharos is one place where Interdict questions may be argued, but it did not
build The Rehearsal, host the Apsis firing, or acquire exclusive authority over
the resulting law. No universal Interdict police or single headquarters is
established by present canon.

## Limits

The Interdict can interrupt construction, deny a firing geometry, isolate a
site, and prevent a suspected fragment from being transported. It cannot certify
that no fragment has ever escaped, inspect every cosmic ray, or reverse a
supercritical strangelet Succession after viable daughter seeds have reached
several causally separated reservoirs.

Its central legal judgment is therefore severe but finite:

> No right to experiment includes the right to make every later refusal
> physically irrelevant.

## Deliberately unresolved

- the full list of original signatories;
- which jurisdictions recognize the architectural-capacity test;
- who may authorize remote entry into Apsis;
- what evidentiary threshold triggers candidate-fragment quarantine;
- whether any state retains a classified defensive firing model; and
- how courts remedy wrongful quarantine when the suspected fragment proves
  ordinary.
