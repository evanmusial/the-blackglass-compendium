# Articles of Open Radiance

## Status and purpose

The Articles of Open Radiance are the principal legal framework governing
projected radiant-return enclosures, military closure anchors, and the right of
inhabited systems to shed waste heat.

Their initial multinational form is adopted in Compact Year +64, three years
after the Closed Night at Ninth Holding. Later jurisdictions expand inspection
and rescue provisions without turning the Articles into one universal code.

Their central rule is that a viable enclosure must possess independently
verifiable **thermal egress**. Heat rejection is treated as life support rather
than an optional efficiency service when people cannot leave safely.

The Articles are separate from the Sixth Compact. Closed Night engineering does
not attack cohesion, and lawful civil radiant-return equipment remains common
under bounded designs.

## Prohibited acts

The Articles prohibit:

- projecting a complete direction-selective radiant boundary around an occupied
  target, regardless of consent;
- projecting an incomplete or narrow-band boundary around an occupied target
  without informed consent, local revocability, and independent inspection;
- possessing covert military closure anchors or disguising them as ordinary
  navigation, lighting, or thermal hardware;
- designing a crewed habitat whose only heat exit can be closed from a coupled
  exterior authority;
- obstructing a rescue attempt to establish a material heat path or evacuation
  corridor;
- and testing wide-band enclosure against unaware occupants, even when modeled
  exposure remains below predicted lethality.

Civil heat-recovery shells remain lawful only when they are physically
incomplete and their return bands, power source, failure state, locally
revocable bypass, and egress capacity can be inspected independently.

Small-source research has a distinct exception: nonliving sources inside
nonoccupiable apparatus with hard energy limits, independent exterior shielding,
and mechanically destructive failsafes. Physical disassembly must prevent
reclosure before inspection. This permits a sealed calibration experiment
without permitting a sealed inhabited chamber; consent does not waive the latter
prohibition.

## What an egress inspection establishes

A visible gap is insufficient if no inhabited heat loop can reach it. Inspectors
trace heat from occupied districts through pumps, valves, stores, and emitting
surfaces to cold space or another adequate external sink. They record the heat
load the path can actually carry after a local failure, the power it requires,
and who can close each part. A reserve radiator connected through the same
failed pump as the main radiator is not an independent egress path.

The certificate belongs to an arrangement, not permanently to a device. Adding
a dock, moving a service skin, installing another company's inspection vanes,
or changing a control contract can invalidate it. Mutual interference among
otherwise lawful operators is a recurring source of disputes: each may preserve
its own aperture while the combined geometry covers both.

Responsibility follows control and capacity. Owners finance ordinary upkeep;
operators document profile changes; residents retain the local bypass; and an
inspector with a separate acquisition path verifies the claim. Signatories
disagree over penalties and compensation, but none can establish compliance
merely by showing that occupants signed a lease acknowledging heat risk.

## Rescue authority

Once hostile closure is credibly established, rescue authorities may destroy
suspected anchors, interrupt the boundary, attach conductive tethers, or create
a path for hot-mass ejection without waiting for ordinary property or signal
authorization.

That authority carries a duty to model a possible **white seam**. A narrow breach
can become the only available radiative exit and expose rescuers to the target's
accumulated thermal burden. Opening a path is therefore not automatically a safe
act, and illuminating or probing an enclosure can add energy to the population
being rescued.

The duty distinguishes ordinary aperture emission from active concentration by
surviving anchors and failing stores. It does not presume that all heat becomes
light at once or that every small breach is a white seam. Rescue records preserve
the assumptions used because a reasonable broad-release decision can still
damage property without constituting negligent enclosure.

## Relationship to managed environments

Avara's older principle that withdrawal of a viable atmosphere is coercive power
supports, but does not subsume, the Articles. Air and thermal egress are distinct
services with distinct failure physics. The legal analogy is that whoever can
withdraw either one exercises power over continued life.

## Jurisdictional boundary

The Articles do not regulate all insulation, mirrors, furnaces, communications
blackouts, or ordinary siege. They apply when engineered direction-selective
transport prevents an occupied system from exporting radiation on which its
thermal survival depends.
