# Human Arrival in Kelan–Ruva

## Status of this history

Humanity originated on Earth and entered the
[Kelan–Ruva system](../places/kelan-ruva-system.md) from elsewhere. Humans did
not evolve independently on [Kharos](../places/kharos.md),
[Avara](../places/avara.md), or another body in the system. Their arrival was a
deliberate settlement effort involving people, reproductive material, food
ecologies, microorganisms, fabrication systems, medical knowledge, and
infrastructure intended to outlive the vehicles that carried them.

That is established authorial canon. It does not mean that every detail is
equally demonstrable to a present-day historian. The Compendium's distinction
between authorial truth and in-world evidence is especially important here.
People in the setting possess overwhelming evidence that humanity and its
managed ecology came from another biosphere. They possess strong evidence that
the remembered cradle was Earth in the Solar System. They do not possess a
complete passenger manifest, a trustworthy departure chronology, a recoverable
transit method, or an observation that converts the old astronomical epoch into
a numbered Earth civil year.

The long period beginning with the first uncrewed survey objects and encompassing
the inhabited arrival assemblages, First Shelter, Heavy Descent, and emergence
of self-sustaining locally born institutions is called the **Founding
Interval**. The last generally accepted arrival assemblage is one of its closing
anchors rather than its sole endpoint. The interval occurred at least several
millennia before the Meridian Age. Its internal sequence is recoverable in broad
and sometimes fine detail, but its first and last years cannot be stated exactly
even in local terms. No true Common Era, Gregorian, or other Earth-calendar year
exists behind the text as a fact known only to an omniscient editor. The missing
correlation is a permanent part of the setting.

The simplest customary statement is:

> We know the sky. We do not know the year.

The more formal scholarly version is:

> The stars preserve intervals and physical epochs. They do not preserve the
> civil name of an epoch.

## Established core

Future histories may add detail without contradicting the following facts:

1. Human beings are descendants of Earth life.
2. The terrestrial biological package entered Kelan–Ruva abruptly on geological
   timescales. No local hominin ancestry precedes it.
3. Settlement was planned rather than an accidental landing by one disabled
   craft.
4. Uncrewed survey machinery entered the system before the oldest accepted
   human occupation.
5. More than one materially distinct inhabited arrival assemblage reached the
   system.
6. Avara contains the oldest accepted continuously occupied human site in
   Kelan–Ruva and was inhabited before Kharos.
7. Kharos was settled through a prolonged sequence of surveys, sealed stations,
   difficult surface residence, and multigenerational expansion. There was no
   single instantaneous founding of the present home world.
8. The original arrival structures were used as infrastructure and feedstock.
   Their disappearance is principally the result of ordinary settlement,
   conversion, repair, and decay rather than one convenient archive-destroying
   catastrophe.
9. Contact with Earth ended before the beginning of a continuous shared local
   chronology.
10. Present scholars can identify Sol's astronomical direction and reconstruct
    old observations from the Solar viewpoint, subject to ordinary uncertainty
    and light-travel delay. No known navigable route reaches Earth.
11. The physical mechanism, duration, and subjective experience of the founding
    passage remain unknown. No current route is proven to use the same method.
12. Earth's condition when the settlers left, their reason for leaving, and
    Earth's subsequent fate remain unresolved.

The Founding Interval predates the documented human development of the four
known-human engineering lineages presently central to the Compendium and the
modern instrumented study of the Manifold. Nothing in the arrival record
establishes a causal connection to the Rzez, the Unhidden, the Manifold, the
Closed Night, or the Agreement. In particular, the absence of a recoverable
transit mechanism is not evidence for a Manifold passage.

## The evidence of terrestrial origin

### The arrival horizon

Across the oldest accessible sediments, sealed cavities, refuse deposits, and
habitation layers, investigators find an abrupt **arrival horizon**. Above that
horizon appear human remains and a linked community of organisms associated
with human survival: crop ancestors, yeasts, food and soil bacteria, gut
symbionts, domestic commensals, pathogens, preserved animal lineages, and
industrial organisms selected for fermentation, waste processing, or chemical
production. Below it, they do not.

The horizon is not identical on every body. Dust deposition on Avara, exposed
materials in orbit, buried Kharosi sites, and closed storage vaults preserve
different parts of the sequence. Later contamination also moved terrestrial
organisms into older cracks and deposits. Nevertheless, independent sites agree
on the central fact: a complicated, already coevolved biological community
arrived over a short interval compared with planetary history.

Human beings fit inside that community. Our cellular machinery, genetic code,
developmental pathways, microbiological dependencies, and diseases share deep
ancestry with the imported organisms. No sequence of local convergent evolution
could explain millions of matching neutral features and inherited biochemical
accidents. Arguments that humanity arose on Kharos and merely imported the rest
of its ecology fail on the same evidence. Humans are one branch of the incoming
tree, not the local custodians of an unrelated collection.

Kelan–Ruva contains no accepted pre-arrival hominin fossils, stone industries,
settlement traces, or evolutionary relatives. Absence alone would not prove an
extrasolar origin, because a fossil record is never complete. Combined with the
positive biological and archaeological horizon, it makes independent local
human evolution untenable.

### The Solar diagrams

Several artifact traditions describe the ancestral system. None is complete,
but they are physically and textually independent.

One family consists of simplified teaching diagrams: a dominant yellow-white
star, a small inner sequence of planets, a third rocky world, and one large moon
relative to its planet. Another consists of engineering tables preserving
orbital-period ratios and reference fluxes. A third appears in ecological
archives whose lighting, seasonal, tidal, and reproductive schedules assume a
roughly terrestrial day, year, and lunar month. Fragmentary geographical images
show one planet with oceans and continental outlines that recur in otherwise
unrelated media.

Any one class could be allegory, copied fiction, or a generic mission model.
Together they identify the Solar System with high confidence. Their numbers fit
the architecture of Sol far better than Kelan–Ruva's close binary, six
circumbinary planets, and two large Kharosi moons. They also explain why ancient
human biological and engineering standards preserve cycles that are awkward in
the local system.

### The Origin Sky

The most technically important evidence is the **Origin Sky**, a composite
reconstruction made from damaged navigation catalogs.

The catalogs contain stellar direction vectors, spectra, proper motions, radial
velocities, binary solutions, pulsar templates, and calibration fields. Their
coordinate labels and source manifests are incomplete, but the observations do
not describe a sky centered on Kelan–Ruva. When modern astrometry propagates the
measured stars backward and searches for the location from which the catalog's
angles and parallaxes make sense, the solutions converge on Sol.

The reconstruction does more than find a familiar constellation pattern.
Constellations are culturally selected and can survive as copied art after their
original geometry has changed. The Origin Sky instead uses the three-dimensional
motions of many stars, multiple stellar systems, and periodic sources. A false
viewpoint may match a few bright stars; it does not match the parallax,
perspective, motion, and timing residuals of the complete surviving set.

The [Kelan–Ruva Concordance Service](../institutions/kelan-ruva-concordance-service.md)
maintains physically independent Origin Sky solutions on Kharos, Avara, and
[Odran](../places/odran.md). Their deliberate independence matters. A single
normalized catalog could silently preserve one historical transcription error.
Separate reductions allow scholars to identify which conclusions follow from
the observations and which depend on a disputed label or correction.

The reconstructed Solar direction is an astronomical result, not a transport
map. No present route terminus is identified with it. Optical observation also
shows a past state because light requires time to cross the intervening space.
Current canon does not assign the distance from Kelan–Ruva to Sol, the resolving
power of any particular observatory, or what may presently be visible of Earth.

### The name Earth

Forms reconstructable as *Earth*, *Terra*, “world,” and “old ground” occur in
different linguistic and material traditions. The word *earth* by itself would
be weak evidence: many cultures call soil and the inhabited world by the same
term. Its repeated association with the Solar diagrams, the Origin Sky, human
ancestry, and inherited geographical images makes the identification strong.

Scholars continue to dispute whether *Earth* was the universal name used by the
departing population, one language's term elevated by later translators, or a
technical endonym selected for historical convenience. That linguistic dispute
does not reopen the biological conclusion. The Compendium may call the cradle
Earth without asserting that every founder did.

## Evidence ladder

Historical grades attach to claims, not to this history as a whole.

### Grade A

Physical remains, independent technical records, and credible contemporary
evidence support the following:

- humans and their managed biological community are not native to Kelan–Ruva;
- the incoming organisms share one older terrestrial evolutionary history;
- uncrewed survey objects precede accepted human occupation;
- settlement cargo was selected for long-term habitation rather than short
  visitation;
- Avara's First Shelter stratum predates permanent Kharosi settlement;
- distinct arrival assemblages introduced differing construction practices and
  additional biological material;
- and the broad survey, orbital refuge, Avaran occupation, Kharosi descent, and
  later expansion sequence is real.

### Grade B

Strong documentary and technical evidence, with incomplete or inaccessible
material remains, supports the following:

- the ancestral biosphere was Earth in the Solar System;
- the old navigation viewpoint was Sol;
- the inhabited assemblages were elements of a deliberate migration program or
  several related programs;
- at least some founders understood themselves to have departed Earth rather
  than merely inheriting Earth records in another colony;
- and later assemblages reached an existing Kelan–Ruva settlement rather than
  forming unrelated settlements rediscovered afterward.

### Grade C

Multiple traditions agree while material interpretation remains incomplete:

- all major assemblages belonged to one distributed convoy;
- the first inhabited assemblage traveled directly from Earth without an
  intermediate settlement;
- particular surface ruins correspond to named settlements in founding songs;
- and one of the major calendar-crosswalk families preserves the correct order
  of Earth departures.

### Grade D

Single substantial sources or clusters of dependent sources support claims that:

- the migration fled a specific war, ecological emergency, persecution, or
  predicted astronomical disaster;
- a founding government promised to reopen the route;
- route records or calendar keys were deliberately destroyed;
- one late assemblage was refused landing and forced to negotiate at Avara;
- or an intermediate colony still exists outside the known route network.

### Grade E

Folklore, political myth, devotional tradition, and propaganda supply stories
that Earth still governs humanity, that Earth watches Kelan–Ruva, that the
founders were exiles selected for moral failure, that the system was promised to
them by a nonhuman intelligence, or that a return fleet will arrive when the
true year is spoken.

These stories may be socially or narratively consequential. They do not settle
Earth's condition, the founders' motive, or the passage mechanism.

## The Founding Interval

### The Far Survey

The oldest recognized human-made objects in the system are uncrewed. Dead
resource assays, navigation reflectors, radiation monitors, and survey packages
occur from the [Ravel Reach](../places/kelan-ruva-system.md#ravel-reach) inward
toward Istra and Vaskor. They do not form a perfect chain, and some objects may
have been displaced, recovered, or redeployed. As a population they reveal a
deliberate reconnaissance sequence.

The surveyors measured the central binary's activity, planetary masses,
volatile inventories, accessible small bodies, atmospheric chemistry,
radiation, and potential biological hazards. Several carried sample chambers or
instrument interfaces whose empty geometry implies collection by later craft.
Some appear to have transmitted inward. Others were designed to retain records
until physically visited.

Their placement shows that the settlers evaluated an entire system. Kelan and
Ruva's close orbit, the destabilized inner cavity, the accessible belts, and the
resources of the outer worlds were not surprises discovered after a desperate
landing. The migration had enough information and operational continuity to
compare alternatives.

The elapsed interval between the Far Survey and the first people remains
uncertain. Internal counters cannot be translated directly, and exposure-age
estimates depend on shielding histories. It may have been measured in years or
decades by the surveyors' proper time. Longer interpretations remain possible.
The canon requires only that the survey preceded accepted human occupation.

### The carrier assemblages

No complete founding vessel survives. Archaeologists use **carrier assemblage**
for a related family of orbital structures, detachable habitats, descent craft,
ecological stores, industrial machinery, and human remains. The term does not
assume that every component crossed interstellar distance as one ship.

At least three inhabited assemblages are required by present evidence. Their
materials use compatible but nonidentical standards. Their archives preserve
different linguistic mixtures. Their biological stores contain overlapping but
distinct subsets of terrestrial diversity. Some components were designed to
connect; others were joined by local adapters. This can describe one program
distributed across many launches, several programs using inherited standards,
or settlers arriving through an intermediate human system.

Population genetics shows that the founding inheritance represented many more
people than the number of adults initially present on the surface. Stored
gametes, embryos, tissue libraries, and lineage records widened the reproductive
base. The surviving evidence does not establish how many passengers were awake,
whether any were placed in long-duration suspension, or whether generations
lived during passage. No passenger method is canonized.

The carriers were not warships accidentally repurposed as towns. Their cargo
priorities include closed-ecology redundancy, obstetric and pediatric medicine,
soil and fermentation systems, machine tools, raw feedstock, educational
archives, and multiple incompatible ways to make food. Weapons and defensive
systems may have been present, but they do not explain the design.

The missing portion of each assemblage is equally important. Surviving systems
begin at habitat power, thermal control, fabrication, maneuver, or descent.
Nothing accepted as a transit stage remains sufficiently complete to explain
the passage. Such stages may have been expended, detached, left at an approach,
destroyed during operation, or unlike any component that entered ordinary local
service. This absence preserves uncertainty; it does not authorize an exotic
answer without evidence.

### First Shelter on Avara

The oldest accepted continuously occupied human site is the **First Shelter
stratum** on Avara. The term applies to an archaeological complex rather than a
preserved founding name. The oldest inscriptions at the site do not supply one
secure endonym, and later cultures applied several incompatible sacred names to
it.

Avara offered a prudent first refuge. Its approximately 0.46 g surface gravity
reduced the immediate load of recovery, medical assessment, and adaptation to a
new system without requiring historians to know what passengers experienced in
transit. Its water and accessible volatiles supported life support and industry.
Its shallow gravity well made repeated orbital exchange less costly than
operations from Kharos. Large structures could be assembled, inspected, and
repaired before the settlers committed a population to the heavier planet.

The first shelters were sealed. This does not mean Avara was airless. It already
possessed water and a substantial secondary volatile inventory, but its long
solar day, atmospheric variability, uncertain local chemistry, and possible
biology justified caution. Human activity did not create a breathable moon from
vacuum during the Founding Interval. It stabilized, supplemented, monitored, and
increasingly occupied an environment that already existed.

Whether Avara possessed an independent biosphere before people arrived remains
unresolved. Some early assay fragments are consistent with local biochemical
activity; others can be explained by contamination or nonliving chemistry. The
human-arrival history must not decide that question by casually calling Avara
sterile or naturally Earthlike.

The first settlement drew directly on orbital structures. Pressure vessels
became water stores. Shielding became walls. Landing frames became cranes and
structural trusses. The material continuity between carrier and town explains
why so little remains in recognizable ship form.

Avara's antiquity has political consequence. It was the first durable human
home in Kelan–Ruva, not a later Kharosi colony. Present uncertainty about its
formal sovereignty cannot erase the settlement sequence.

### Surveying the heavy world

Kharos was the obvious long-term prize and the more difficult immediate home. It
had a vast surface, deep atmosphere, abundant water and mineral resources, and
approximately Earthlike mean stellar flux. It also imposed approximately 1.54 g
at the surface, far greater entry and launch costs, and biological uncertainty
on a population with no secure replacement.

Robotic and short-duration crewed surveys therefore preceded permanent surface
residence. The earliest Kharosi installations were sealed coastal, lacustrine,
and upland stations supported from orbit and Avara. Their locations reflect
access to water, manageable weather, landing safety, and the ability to perform
work with buoyant or recumbent support. High elevation offered only a negligible
reduction in gravity; it was chosen for climate, access, or atmospheric reasons,
not as a fictional escape from Kharosi weight.

Biological screening was prolonged. A breathable atmosphere is not the same as
a safe environment or proof of a living one. Settlers tested local aerosols,
reactive compounds, possible microorganisms, allergens, toxins, and the
ecological effects of terrestrial introductions. The record supports increasing
open-air work but does not settle whether Kharos possessed prehuman life or, if
it did, that life's origin and history.

### The Heavy Descent

The transition to permanent Kharosi settlement is called the **Heavy Descent**.
It was not one footprint, one landing day, or one leader's decree. It was the
period in which surface stations stopped being temporary, children were raised
under Kharosi gravity, local food and material cycles became reliable, and
evacuation to Avara ceased to be the assumed answer to every serious failure.

The human cost was substantial. Founding medical records describe circulatory
stress, loss of mobility, fractures, pregnancy complications, difficult births,
and long rehabilitation. Later memorial traditions compress those losses into
heroic tales of people standing unaided on the first day. The physical evidence
shows more restraint: settlers used low buildings, abundant lifting machinery,
water-supported therapy, powered assistance, scheduled recumbence, aggressive
cardiovascular care, and gradual exposure.

No single biological modification made Kharos possible. Present humanity may
reflect long selection, inherited medical practice, prenatal conditioning,
assistive systems, and changes whose histories are lost, but canon does not
assign a universal high-gravity gene or a distinct Kharosi subspecies. Human
bodies remain recognizably descended from Earth bodies, and dependence on
infrastructure is an ordinary part of Kharosi life rather than evidence of
failure.

Kharos eventually exceeded Avara in population and civic reach. Its stable
hydrosphere, atmosphere, resources, and habitable area supported growth that
Avara's smaller, actively maintained environment could not match. Kharos became
the familiar home world of the setting through settlement, demography,
institutions, and return, not through biological origin or priority.

### The later arrivals

Later inhabited assemblages reached a society already established on Avara and
in the early stages of Kharosi expansion. Their existence is supported by sudden
changes in construction standards, new archive dialects, additional terrestrial
lineages, and repairs that join locally manufactured components to arriving
hardware.

The new populations did not enter an empty system. They negotiated access to
air, water, descent capacity, land, reproductive infrastructure, and political
membership. Later chronicles remember welcomes, quarantines, refusals, and one
near conflict, but no complete account is Grade A. The material record does show
that later people and biological stores were incorporated rather than excluded
as a permanent population.

These arrivals explain why the earliest society never possessed one uncontested
Earth tradition. Different groups carried different languages, civil calendars,
religions, technical conventions, political memories, and departure stories.
They also carried Earth records that may have been copied before departure,
during an intermediate settlement, or from other archives. An authentic Earth
document in a late carrier does not prove that the carrier itself departed
Earth.

The later assemblages are one reason claims of pure founder descent have little
scientific basis. Reproductive archives broadened ancestry further, and later
movement mixed the populations. A present family may preserve a founding name
without possessing an exclusive biological line to one arriving passenger.

### The useful destruction of the carriers

There was no single night in which every ship, archive, and calendar burned.
The founding vehicles disappeared because a settlement uses what it has.

Radiation shielding became foundations and storm shelters. Pressure hulls became
reservoirs, clinics, and workshops. Power systems were rebuilt until no original
assembly remained. Computation substrates were repurposed, repaired with local
components, migrated into new formats, and finally failed. Ecological stores
became fields, vats, forests, herds, laboratories, and bodies. Rare alloys passed
through several generations of machines before entering anonymous scrap.

This process was rational. The settlers needed housing and life support more
than monuments to vehicles they could not reproduce. Redundant archives were
often judged safe to copy while the obsolete hardware holding their metadata
was discarded. Flight software that transformed coordinates and clocks had
little daily use once local navigation became dominant. Human-facing texts,
images, recipes, and teaching materials survived more readily because people
kept reading and copying them.

There were accidents, conflicts, data losses, and deliberate suppressions
during the long foundation. None is required to carry the entire explanatory
burden. The central tragedy is ordinary: the settlers preserved what kept them
alive, and many things that would fascinate their descendants did not appear
essential at the time.

### Severance from Earth

Contact with Earth ended before the beginning of a continuous shared local
chronology. **The Severance** is a later historical name for that condition, not
an established single event.

No accepted record shows a final exchange in which both parties understood that
contact would never resume. A last message may have arrived without being
recognized as last. A return attempt may have failed beyond observation. A
route may have closed gradually. Infrastructure may have become unusable at
either end. Political authorities may have chosen silence. Earth itself may
have changed or remained ordinary.

Founding traditions frequently turn Severance into a moral scene: a door shut
by cowards, a parent releasing a child, an exile pronounced, a rescue promised,
or a necessary destruction of the past. Those traditions reveal later needs.
They do not establish the physical mechanism or Earth's fate.

The word *route* may be used descriptively for the lost connection, but it does
not establish that founding transit obeyed the present route network. The known
inhabited region contains no recognized Earthward terminus. Surveying the Solar
direction in ordinary space is not equivalent to navigating there.

### The long foundation and regional expansion

After the arrival assemblages ceased, Kharos, Avara, Odran, and orbital society
developed local industries, political communities, and time standards. Human
settlement eventually extended to other systems. Some expansion occurred before
the Meridian Age, although the order and number of those settlements remain
open outside the home system.

By the time Meridian institutions operated across several worlds, Earth was
accepted ancestry rather than accessible administration. Founding languages had
become ancestral languages, liturgical forms, technical roots, or subjects of
reconstruction. Carrier components had become archaeological layers. The old
calendar problem was already a scholarly and political dispute.

This timescale should remain long enough for genuine cultures to form. Kharosi
and Avaran society are not shipboard cultures with renamed rooms. Their relation
to Earth resembles inheritance across a civilizational break: materially real,
emotionally powerful, and incapable of supplying an answer to every present
question.

## The Earth-calendar correlation problem

### Three meanings of “when”

The apparent paradox is simple: if scholars can read the stars, why can they not
state the present Earth year?

The question conflates three different quantities.

1. A **physical interval** measures elapsed proper time or a defined dynamical
   time between observations.
2. An **astronomical epoch** describes the physical configuration of stars,
   pulsars, planets, and reference systems at an observation.
3. A **civil date** assigns a culturally defined label to an interval or epoch.

The first two can be reconstructed without the third. A civil year number is not
a naturally occurring property of the sky. Gregorian year one, a religious era,
a national foundation, a mission epoch, and an onboard counter begin because
people define them. Extending one of those calendars to Kelan–Ruva requires a
record that joins its social zero point to a measurable physical state.

### What the stars reveal

Modern investigators can propagate stellar positions and velocities backward,
fit binary motions, compare pulsar periods and orbital decay, and test old
catalog observations against a reconstructed viewpoint. With enough independent
objects, a catalog can be placed in physical time relative to the present model
far more narrowly than the Founding Interval itself.

The precision varies. Stellar accelerations, unknown companions, pulsar glitches,
catalog transcription, radial-velocity error, and uncertainty in the old
instrument's position broaden the solution. Those are ordinary scientific
limits, not mystical temporal damage. Combining many objects still establishes
the order and approximate age of the surviving catalog families.

Artifact dating provides different intervals. Radiation exposure constrains how
long an object remained outside shielding. Isotope systems date manufacture or
biological death within their ranges. Stratigraphy orders settlement layers.
Introduced ecological successions and locally counted natural cycles add further
constraints. These measurements can show that one carrier predates another or
that a catalog was already old when copied.

None writes “Gregorian” into a spectral line.

### What the archives retain

The old technical archives preserve fragments identifiable as star catalogs,
planetary ephemerides, pulse templates, clock corrections, and coordinate
transformations. Many contain exact-looking numbers. Their numbers are usually
offsets from package-specific epochs or counters intended for software, not
self-describing dates intended for remote descendants.

Human-facing records preserve names of months, weekdays, seasonal observances,
leap rules, numbered dates, centuries, political reigns, religious eras, and
mission days. They show that several terrestrial calendars were in use and that
ships added elapsed-time systems of their own. Some archives preserve enough to
reconstruct the length and rules of a calendar but not enough to locate its
current count.

The difference is crucial. Knowing that one old calendar used approximately
365.2425 terrestrial days and a leap-year rule does not reveal which numbered
year contained a particular stellar observation. Knowing that a navigation
catalog was observed a calculable interval ago does not reveal which civil
calendar its source organization displayed.

### The missing crosswalk

No accepted artifact contains, in one independently authenticated chain:

- an unambiguous Earth civil calendar and era;
- a complete numbered date under that calendar;
- a uniquely recoverable celestial state or physical time coordinate;
- the observation's location and clock convention;
- and the transformations connecting that record through transit to local time.

Records normally contain one side of the bridge. Navigation catalogs carry a
physical epoch in machine form but have lost the manifest defining the epoch
counter or the human calendar label. Diaries and public media carry calendar
dates but lack an independently recoverable sky state, source clock, or proof
that the date describes publication rather than an event being remembered.

Several fragments seem at first to provide both. Each fails under custody or
dependency review. One famous eclipse account is a later quotation without a
secure observing location. A launch image displaying a year has no authenticated
connection to the carrier in whose archive it was found. A pulsar table and
civil timestamp occur in one restored file, but both descend from the same
post-arrival conversion whose source epoch is missing. Exactness is not
independence.

### How ordinary computation lost the bridge

The calendar loss does not require the founders to become scientifically
ignorant.

Navigation software stored time as offsets from defined epochs. Different
systems selected different zeros, scales, and rollover widths. Ship operations
translated source timestamps into mission elapsed time, passenger schedules,
proper time, or a local planning clock. Once a conversion succeeded, downstream
records often retained the converted value rather than the full chain of
definitions.

Repeated migration made this worse. A file copied from Earth to a carrier, from
the carrier to an Avaran archive, into an early Kharosi standard, and then into a
later historical repository could preserve every word while losing filesystem
metadata and clock declarations at each stage. Legacy formats were encapsulated,
decoded, normalized, and discarded. Checksums prove that a surviving payload
matches an earlier copy; they cannot restore a header never included in that
payload.

Some counters wrapped. Some used truncated era fields. Some date renderers
assumed an external locale. Some named standards survive only as initialisms
whose normative definitions are absent. Fragments resembling UTC, TAI, Julian
dates, ISO notation, or religious calendars are historically valuable but do
not form a complete crosswalk merely because present scholars recognize likely
ancestors.

The founders also possessed more than one authentic departure history. A later
carrier could leave Earth after an earlier one, spend a different proper
interval in transit, or arrive through an intermediate settlement. Two clocks
may disagree because they answer different questions. Without the transit
worldline or route transformation, an experienced proper duration does not
specify which Earth-coordinate event should be considered simultaneous with
arrival. This is ordinary relativistic and archival caution; it does not imply
time travel or reversed causality.

### Why historical astronomy does not close the gap

In principle, an authenticated Earth account of a sufficiently distinctive
eclipse, planetary conjunction, or other event could connect a numbered civil
date to a physical sky. No surviving candidate satisfies all necessary
conditions.

Many astronomical references are conventional phrases. Others lack observing
location, time of day, calendar identity, or assurance that the date records the
event rather than a later copy. Over long spans, several configurations can fit
a damaged description. Selecting one because it produces a desired present year
is circular.

The historical epoch underlying an Earth calendar is also not necessarily an
astronomical event. A calendar may count from a religious, political, or
retrospectively assigned date. Reconstructing its leap rules does not reconstruct
the documentary chronology by which a particular Earth society knew its current
year.

Scholars can therefore state a physical age for an old observation, sometimes
with impressive precision, while remaining unable to attach the culturally
correct integer. They know *where in stellar evolution and motion* the
observation belongs. They do not know what an Earth official, household, temple,
or ship display called that year.

### Crosswalk families

Several proposed **crosswalk families** reconcile subsets of the evidence. Some
interpret a damaged counter as having wrapped once; others require several
rollovers. Some treat a four-digit display as a Common Era year; others identify
it as a mission or archival sequence. Some assume that two carrier logs departed
under one calendar; others treat them as records from different Earth societies
or intermediate settlements.

The leading families yield mutually incompatible numbered Earth years separated
by large intervals. Each can be internally rigorous. None has an independent
anchor sufficient to choose it over the others. Several rely on the same early
local conversion table and therefore represent one dependent source despite
their different modern calculations.

No crosswalk claim may presently exceed Grade C. A proposal does not become
Grade A by adding decimal places.

Compendium chronology should use the Founding Interval, stratigraphic sequence,
local physical durations, named historical eras, and Compact-relative dates
where appropriate. It should not place a speculative Common Era year in
parentheses as though that were harmless convenience.

## Competing settlement models

### The One Convoy account

The One Convoy account treats the Far Survey, carrier assemblages, and later
arrivals as elements of one distributed migration. Different craft departed or
arrived on different schedules but shared an organizing authority and intended
settlement design.

The account explains compatible interfaces and overlapping archives. Its
supporters argue that a serious colony program would deliberately distribute
people, ecologies, and equipment so the loss of one craft could not end the
mission. Critics answer that some standards and departure traditions differ too
deeply to represent scheduled variation inside one program.

The central claim is Grade C.

### The Layered Migration model

The Layered Migration model holds that several migrations left Earth at
different times. Early survey or settlement reports may have encouraged later
departures. Some migrations may have been sponsored by different states,
communities, or successor institutions.

This is the most common scholarly account because it explains the authentic
clock disagreements, linguistic diversity, and later biological introductions
without requiring one program to preserve radical internal incompatibility. It
still leaves open whether the migrations coordinated or knew of one another.

The existence of materially distinct layers is Grade A. Interpreting them as
independent Earth departures is Grade B to C depending on the assemblage.

### The Relay model

The Relay model proposes that at least some founders reached Kelan–Ruva from an
intermediate human settlement. Their Earth archives were inherited, and their
biological ancestry remained terrestrial, but their own departure port was not
Earth.

This model accounts for records that describe Earth with reverence yet show no
sign of firsthand planetary operations. It also permits forgotten inhabited
systems outside the present route network without increasing the number of
currently connected systems.

No intermediate world has been securely identified. The model is Grade C.

### The Refuge account

Refuge traditions say the settlers fled war, environmental collapse,
persecution, pandemic, impact, stellar danger, machine rule, or another terminal
threat. Different traditions name mutually incompatible disasters.

Emergency language occurs in maintenance manuals, nursery lessons, and one
damaged departure address. That language proves that danger was imaginable, not
that Earth was ending. A planned self-sufficient migration would carry emergency
doctrine under peaceful conditions.

Specific refuge claims are Grade D or E. Earth's fate remains open.

### Deliberate Severance

The deliberate-severance school argues that founding authorities destroyed
route and calendar records to prevent old governments, creditors, religions,
castes, or departure cohorts from claiming permanent authority. Some versions
describe emancipation; others describe a crime by the first Avaran leadership.

The theory draws strength from later no-priority law and from a few archives
whose omissions appear selective. It draws weakness from the lack of a
destruction order and the adequacy of ordinary data migration, component reuse,
and multiple arrivals to explain the record.

The claim is Grade D. No institution may treat it as established guilt.

### The accidental-colony minority

A minority school argues that the carriers intended another destination or a
temporary stop and remained only after transit failure. The breadth of survey
and settlement cargo makes pure accident implausible. Kelan–Ruva may have been
one acceptable destination among several, but it was evaluated for durable
occupation.

The claim that settlement itself was unintended is contradicted by Grade A
evidence.

## Cultural consequences

### Home without cradle

Kharos is called home without being called humanity's place of origin. The
distinction is ordinary, not a technical correction recited only by scholars.
People may be fully Kharosi while acknowledging that the human lineage began on
Earth and first survived locally on Avara.

This allows the setting to hold three truths at once:

- Earth is the ancestral cradle;
- Avara is the first durable human shelter in Kelan–Ruva;
- and Kharos is the principal present home world.

None cancels the others.

### Avara's first-shelter tradition

Avaran political culture treats viable environment as shared infrastructure in
part because its oldest society lived inside deliberately maintained air,
water, heat, and pressure systems. Founding stories rarely describe possession
of land first. They describe keeping a volume alive together.

First Shelter therefore supports Avara's claim to coequal standing. Kharosi
population and later legal centrality do not make the moon a historical
dependency. Political movements may exaggerate the antiquity into moral
superiority, but the underlying settlement order is real.

### The Heavy Descent in Kharosi memory

Kharosi traditions celebrate persistence under weight. Responsible histories
emphasize assistance, medicine, and collective infrastructure. Heroic versions
replace those systems with solitary founders who stood unaided, carried stones,
or refused evacuation.

The distinction has present moral force. Disability advocates, clinicians, and
labor organizations cite the archaeological record against the claim that
dependence on support makes a person less authentically Kharosi. Everyone's
ancestors depended on machinery, care, and one another during the Heavy
Descent.

### Earth-return traditions

Returnists range from astronomers seeking an ordinary probe mission to religions
that regard physical return as humanity's unfinished obligation. Some preserve
Solar diagrams in worship. Others reject all proposed routes as dangerous
counterfeits.

Severance traditions take the opposite position. They teach that humanity
became responsible for itself when Earth ceased to answer and that attempting to
restore old authority would betray the founding. Neither family possesses
special evidence about Earth's present state.

### The unknown anniversary

There is no universally defensible Arrival Day. Communities commemorate First
Shelter, the beginning of a local archive, a later legal foundation, or a
seasonal symbolic date. Ceremonies often acknowledge this openly by leaving one
clock face blank, beginning with an unnumbered interval, or reading several
incompatible founding dates without selecting one.

An “Earth year” may survive as a reconstructed physical duration in education,
biology, or historical comparison. It must be distinguished from an Earth
calendar year number. The former can be measured; the latter requires the lost
crosswalk.

### Language of inheritance

Common expressions include “before shelter” for events beyond dependable local
history, “under first weight” for an obligation accepted without certainty, and
“a crosswalk year” for a claim whose precision exceeds its evidence.

“Earthborn” usually refers to biological inheritance or an ancient artifact,
not to a living person's birthplace. Claims that a living person was born on
Earth receive the evidentiary scrutiny appropriate to an extraordinary and
route-changing assertion.

## Legal consequences

### No priority of arrival

A broad Kharosi–Avaran principle holds that archaeological seniority does not
create hereditary sovereignty. Descent from an earlier passenger, possession of
a carrier surname, or association with First Shelter grants no superior claim
to land, atmosphere, citizenship, office, or public resources.

The rule developed because later assemblages joined an existing population and
because stored reproductive material made simple passenger genealogies
misleading. It also prevents one newly decoded manifest from retroactively
placing millions of people under a founding contract they never accepted.

The principle does not erase ordinary private property or proven stewardship.
It denies political dominion based solely on arriving first.

### Calendar neutrality

Courts and archives must state the time basis of consequential dates. Earth
civil calendars, mission elapsed time, Kharosi orbital counts, Avaran cycles,
institutional reckonings, and Compact Years may not be silently converted.

A proposed Earth crosswalk cannot determine legal age, inheritance, treaty
priority, limitation periods, citizenship, or ownership unless its relevant
anchor is independently established. At present, no global crosswalk meets that
standard. Exact-looking historical dates may be admitted as content of an old
record while their conversion remains disputed.

This doctrine prevents calendar scholarship from becoming an instrument of
mass dispossession. It also makes uncertainty explicit instead of forcing every
archive into one convenient chronology.

### The common inheritance of arrival material

Surviving carrier structures, ecological repositories, genomic libraries,
navigation catalogs, and early settlement records are treated as a common human
inheritance even when a particular institution holds them.

The rule is practical as well as symbolic. One damaged header could alter the
history of an introduced pathogen, a reproductive lineage, or the Origin Sky.
Destructive analysis, exclusive suppression, and removal from the system require
review. Replication is encouraged, but copies must preserve variant states and
custody rather than silently harmonizing them.

The [Institute of Applied Continuity](../institutions/institute-of-applied-continuity.md)
may study such material, but it does not own the human origin record. The
Concordance Service may authenticate independent copies, but it is not a court
of ancestral legitimacy.

### No inherited Earth jurisdiction

No fragmentary Earth charter, state claim, corporate instrument, religious
decree, or passenger contract governs the present merely because it is old.
Such documents may establish what founders believed or agreed among themselves.
Their authority did not automatically survive Severance, later arrivals,
generational settlement, and the creation of present polities.

This boundary does not require contempt for Earth law. Many legal traditions
openly descend from terrestrial sources. It distinguishes intellectual and moral
inheritance from an enforceable command by an unreachable authority.

## Institutional practice

### The Concordance Service

The Kelan–Ruva Concordance Service maintains separate Origin Sky reductions,
arrival-artifact clocks, and crosswalk dependency graphs on Kharos, Avara, and
Odran. A proposed correlation must identify which observations are independent
and which descend from one copied conversion.

The Service can certify that two records are authentic yet noncomparable. It can
also show that five supposedly independent calendar solutions inherit one
damaged epoch table. Its role is evidentiary. It cannot declare an Earth year by
institutional vote.

### Odran's reference role

Odran's vacuum observatories and physically separated archives are well suited
to refining stellar motions and preserving variant datasets. The moon does not
possess a master clock whose authority solves the founding problem. Its value is
independence, stable observation, and custody outside the continuous utilities
of Kharos or Avara.

### The Institute tradition

Scholars in the Institute of Applied Continuity tradition study biological
lineage, material custody, ancient computation, and the legal continuity of
founding claims. Their involvement makes the human-arrival history one of the
oldest continuity disputes, but it does not imply that arrival and the five
dangerous systems share a mechanism.

Institute critics warn that the desire for one elegant timeline can become a
form of evidence destruction. A reconstruction that forces every authentic
carrier record into one calendar may be less truthful than an archive that
preserves several unresolved transformations.

## Consequences for the realistic system

The settlement history should remain accountable to the physical architecture
of Kelan–Ruva.

- The binary was recognized and measured before inhabited arrival. Its
  16.65-day inertial orbit is not a historical surprise or a source of magical
  seasons; the locally observed presentation cycle is approximately 17.63 days.
- Avara was chosen first for low-gravity access, water, volatiles, assembly, and
  refuge. Its atmosphere was managed, not conjured rapidly from vacuum.
- Kharos's approximately 1.54 g gravity imposed genuine medical and industrial
  costs. Culture did not simply ignore it.
- The outer belts and giant planets supplied survey baselines, volatiles,
  feedstock, and navigation infrastructure before they supported any extensive
  permanent population.
- Odran became valuable later as an airless, separated reference environment;
  it was not the easiest first home for a biologically vulnerable population.
- The close binary and circumbinary planets do not prevent precise astronomy.
  They require a barycentric ephemeris and careful local clock definitions.
- The ability to determine local orbital phases to extraordinary precision does
  not recover a missing Earth civil epoch.

The natural system existed for billions of years before humanity. Human history
occupies a thin recent layer within it. Naming, settlement, atmospheric
management, and industry changed the lived system without creating its stars,
planets, moons, belts, or basic dynamics.

## Hard boundaries

Future work must preserve the following unresolved matters unless a deliberate
canon revision replaces this history:

- Do not assign a numbered Earth departure, arrival, or present year.
- Do not provide a secret authorial conversion to Common Era, Gregorian, Julian,
  or another terrestrial civil calendar.
- Do not establish the journey's duration from either an Earth or passenger
  perspective.
- Do not choose suspended animation, generation travel, relativistic flight,
  gates, corridors, engineered space, living ships, or another transit method.
- Do not identify present interstellar routes as descendants of the founding
  passage without a separate setting decision.
- Do not connect the arrival to the Manifold merely because the route is lost.
- Do not establish Earth's survival, destruction, habitability, political
  condition, or knowledge of Kelan–Ruva.
- Do not establish one motive for migration.
- Do not fix the total number, names, sponsors, or complete passenger counts of
  the carrier assemblages.
- Do not require every assemblage to have departed directly from Earth.
- Do not make Kelan–Ruva humanity's first extrasolar settlement unless later
  regional history earns that claim.
- Do not make Kharos the cradle of humanity or Avara a Kharosi colony.
- Do not turn Avara from vacuum into a breathable world on an implausibly short
  founding timescale.
- Do not settle whether Avara possessed an independent biosphere.
- Do not settle whether Kharos possessed a prehuman biosphere.
- Do not invent a single genetic adaptation that makes Kharosi gravity
  effortless.
- Do not grant political priority, purity, or superior personhood to descendants
  of an alleged first carrier.
- Do not treat an exact date as strong merely because its arithmetic is precise.

What is established should remain emotionally firm. Human beings came from
Earth. They carried a whole living inheritance rather than an abstract genome.
They surveyed Kelan–Ruva, made their first durable shelter on Avara, accepted the
cost of settling heavy Kharos, received later arrivals, dismantled their vehicles
to build a civilization, and lost the ability to speak to the old home.

They know where their ancestral sky was observed. They can measure how the stars
have moved. They can reconstruct intervals, sequence ruins, and expose false
precision. What they cannot recover is the one social bridge that would let them
say what Earth called the year.
