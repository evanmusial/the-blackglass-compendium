# Avara

## Role in the home system

Avara is the large outer moon of [Kharos](kharos.md) and the home system's oldest
continuously occupied human world. Earth-originating arrivals maintained a refuge
here before durable settlement on Kharos survived. Ordinary and Compact records
call Avara a moon, although the Kharos–Avara barycenter lies outside Kharos and
their dynamics resemble a binary-planet pair.

Avara is not humanity's cradle. Its historical seniority is local to the
Kelan–Ruva system, and it does not imply that the first arrival, first landing,
and beginning of continuous occupation were one event. Odran also preserves
infrastructure from the broad founding interval. What distinguishes Avara is the
unbroken human refuge, not necessarily the oldest surviving manufactured object.

The oldest occupation is called the
[**First Shelter stratum**](../history/human-arrival-in-kelan-ruva.md#first-shelter-on-avara).
That is an archaeological term, not a recovered settlement name.

That distinction carries political weight. Avara is not merely a Kharosi suburb:
it has its own settlements, institutions, dependencies, and interests. Its
sovereignty and the limits of Kharosi jurisdiction remain unresolved.

Avaran civic doctrine begins from an unavoidable material fact: breathable air
is maintained common infrastructure. Authorities may ration, isolate, or repair
an atmospheric district, but withdrawal of a person's viable environment is an
exercise of coercive power subject to review. This principle predates the
arrival of autonomous open forms and gives Avara an independent basis for
humane-custody law.

## Physical condition

Avara has a mean diameter of approximately 6,250 kilometres, a mass of
approximately 0.11 Earth masses by authorial comparison, and surface gravity
near 0.46 g. It orbits Kharos at approximately 650,000 kilometres every 36.67
days and is tidally locked to the planet. Its mean solar day is approximately
41.8 days.

Its oceans and substantial atmosphere redistribute heat across that long day.
The breathable atmosphere is secondary rather than an untouched remnant of the
system's formation. It is continuously measured and replenished by present
infrastructure. Avara is genuinely habitable, but its habitability is a managed
public achievement rather than invisible background.

## Fixed sky and long day

Tidal locking divides Avara into two permanent geographic relationships with
Kharos. From the near hemisphere, Kharos remains fixed in the sky and appears
approximately 1.46 degrees across. It changes phase during the long solar day but
does not rise or set. From the far hemisphere, the planet is never visible.

The difference is practical before it is symbolic. Near-side sites can maintain
direct optical paths to Kharos when weather and local terrain permit. Far-side
sites require orbital, horizon, or surface relays and can more easily isolate
themselves from Kharosi radio and optical spill. This does not fix where most
people live or which hemisphere holds political power. It does make "in sight of
Kharos" a literal engineering condition rather than a metaphor.

Kharos changes from a narrow illuminated edge during much of the local day to a
large bright disk during the local night. Planetshine can soften near-side
darkness; the far side has no equivalent source. In favorable eclipse seasons,
Kharos also carries one or both stars across part of the near-side sky. Those
events are scheduled power and thermal disturbances, not evidence that Avara is
permanently shadowed.

Away from polar and seasonal complications, daylight and darkness each persist
for roughly half of the 41.8-day cycle. Ocean and atmosphere move heat across
that cycle, but infrastructure must still bridge it. Solar districts store
energy before dusk. Industrial heat stores, water reserves, and weather services
are sized for long transitions rather than a short nightly interruption.
Facilities that cannot survive one dark interval without continuous remote
direction are not treated as locally complete.

The stars themselves retain the system's asymmetry. Kelan dominates illumination;
Ruva produces a smaller red contribution and a weaker second shadow. The paired
presentation changes on the same approximately 17.63-day observed cycle as at
Kharos, with small local parallax superimposed by Avara's orbit around the planet
and its much longer solar day.

## Founding refuge and historical seniority

The oldest continuously occupied human refuge in Kelan–Ruva is on Avara, but no
accepted record fixes its original name, exact arrival date, or founding
authority. Low gravity, water, accessible volatiles, orbital exchange, and room
to assemble and inspect large structures all made the moon a prudent refuge.
The condition of particular arriving assemblages and the weight founders gave
each advantage remain disputed. The relative sequence is fixed; the
single-cause story is not.

Continuous occupation did not mean effortless open-air settlement. Avara's
atmosphere is secondary, its long-term loss cannot be ignored, and every durable
community depends upon measurement, repair, and replenishment. The first shelters
were sealed even though water and a substantial volatile inventory already
existed. Human settlement stabilized and supplemented that environment; it did
not manufacture a breathable moon rapidly from vacuum. The earliest habitable
systems have been replaced many times. Modern machinery should not be mistaken
for evidence that habitability began recently, and ancient occupation should not
be mistaken for proof that the present atmosphere is primordial, wholly
artificial, or biologically sterile.

Durable Kharosi settlement later exceeded Avara in population and institutional
scale. The demographic shift did not empty Avara. It changed the older refuge
into a partner in transport, medicine, industry, and environmental practice.
By the middle-to-late Meridian Age, Avaran surface works and orbital yards were
part of a mature triworld economy with Kharos and Odran. Political claims built
upon that history remain unresolved: age does not automatically establish
sovereignty, and later demographic weight does not erase it.

## Settlement and infrastructure

Permanent settlement is sparse relative to the inhabited surface. Scientific,
industrial, medical, military, and orbital infrastructure is extensive. Large
territories remain unsettled and should not be filled with cities merely because
the moon can support them.

Low gravity changes ordinary competence. Long spans and large work volumes are
less costly to support, and a person has more time to arrest a fall. Inertia does
not become smaller with weight: cargo that is easy to lift can still crush a
worker once moving, and broad structures can accumulate dangerous momentum.
Avaran yards therefore emphasize restraint paths, slow capture, and visible
mass-state indicators rather than treating lightness as harmlessness.

Residents traveling to Kharos require strength, circulatory, and movement
preparation for 1.54 g. The form of that preparation is not fixed as one genetic,
medical, or mechanical adaptation. Visitors arriving from Kharos face the
opposite problem: overpowered movement and misplaced confidence are common
hazards before low-gravity habits become automatic.

Near-side and far-side installations solve different communication and thermal
problems, but neither hemisphere is assigned one social character. Direct view
of Kharos can be useful for traffic and shared time transfer. Relay dependence
and lower planetary interference can be useful for isolated archives,
observatories, and systems intended to retain local authority.

Atmospheric support, water movement, power, and transport are divided into
district-scale systems capable of surviving one another's isolation. This
post-Valec pattern accepts inefficiency in exchange for preventing one Rzez
event, false alarm, or emergency severance from turning a continuous utility
network into a world-wide transmission path.

The segmentation is a later hardening of older managed-habitat practice, not the
beginning of Avaran interdependence. Long before Valec, air and water accounts
already required local measurement and reserve. After Valec, the same systems
were rebuilt so that a district could disconnect without losing every means of
keeping itself alive. The optical backbone remains dense, but shutdown authority
and minimum viable stores remain local.

## Boundary medicine

Avara's low gravity and broad clinical-industrial base make it the natural place
to grow and rehabilitate very large total-boundary scaffolds descended from the
first open wards on Kharos. The moon hosts wet-cradle facilities, tissue-lineage
repositories, and recovery environments in which a mantle can spread a greater
functional area without bearing Kharosi weight.

Gravity is part of the organism's architecture. A broad Avaran recovery mantle,
a Kharos-rated support form, and a compact boarding form cannot be treated as
the same body with different instructions. Trellis density, fluid retention,
circulatory work, stance, and safe remapping all change with the environment in
which the tissue was grown.

Some Avaran facilities can receive cooperative autonomous open forms under
humane-care protocols. Their life support remains wet, chemically legible, and
non-occluding. This does not settle whether a Plain Thing is patient, veteran,
prisoner, citizen, or manufactured person, and it does not place the military
conversion of the lineage on Avara.

## Cooperative control and thermal egress

Avaran orbital industry contributes distributed timing and free-flying return
vanes to the civil lineage later weaponized as The Closed Night. Its principal
defensive contribution is the first mandatory **unreturnable aperture**: a heat
or matter path whose open state cannot be revoked solely by the exterior system
that benefits from closure.

This requirement follows Avara's older viable-environment principle without
conflating air and heat. Atmospheric support and thermal egress have different
physics, but control of either can become coercive power over people who cannot
leave.

In Compact Year +164, the Three-Hand Rescue in Avaran orbit demonstrates bounded
reciprocal control among consenting operators. Later Avaran practice preserves
the limits that made the system civil: a small task, short duration, hard
physical disconnect, exterior variance, and no majority authority over a
participant's body. The event becomes both the humane origin of The Agreement
and the evidence that weaponization was a removal of safeguards rather than an
unrelated discovery.

## Continuity and concordance

Avara is a coequal custodian of the Kelan–Ruva Concordance Service rather than a
remote instrument platform for Kharos. It maintains time standards, ranging
instruments, and archives independent of the planet. Its records are exchanged
through the system's optical backbone only after local collection and
authentication. If local shard conditions or participating equipment implicate
a link in establishment, the moon can sever that link and continue on local
clocks. Distant incident reports are safe to receive.

The same independence detects Agreement contamination. High-throughput links may
exchange records, but safety-critical control loops retain asynchronous local
references and one-way shutdown paths. Kharos–Avara light-time also prevents one
ordinary reciprocal lattice from treating the two worlds as an immediate shared
body.

Atmospheric dependence makes Avara particularly conservative about both Rzez
propagation and hostile open-form action. A countermeasure that dries or seals a
facility may defeat an organism while also threatening patients and the public
systems that keep the moon habitable. Defensive doctrine therefore treats
environmental control as life-support authority, not merely a weapon switch.

## Blackglass environmental ethics

Avaran law and custom treat dangerous translation as an environmental act. A
reader may accept personal risk but cannot consent on behalf of a pressure
district, atmosphere service, wet-cradle population, or vessel whose inhabitants
would share the consequence of successful reconstruction. A fluent Blackglass
rendering can change what nearby people are capable of building, recognizing,
or fearing even before a physical experiment begins.

Custodial rooms therefore use independent air, power, local shutdown, and
outward-only reporting where practical. Their separation is ordinary
information and habitat safety, not proof that the
[Compendium](../../frame/blackglass-tradition.md) can travel through air. Popular
speech nevertheless says that Blackglass must never be allowed to **learn the
air**.

Several First Shelter-derived observances leave one line of a memorial,
inheritance, or founding account deliberately untranslated. The incomplete line
represents the right to survive without completing every inherited instruction.
The custom is older than current Blackglass controls in its modern form and has
been reinterpreted through them rather than established as an original response
to the collection.

## Deliberately unresolved

- Avara's population, government, settlement names, and relationship to any
  wider Kharosi polity;
- whether it possessed an independent biosphere before human activity;
- the exact Earth-origin arrival date, founding route, original First Shelter
  endonym, and responsible arrival assemblage;
- the detailed founding-era atmospheric chemistry and the relative human and
  natural contributions to its later replenishment;
- the builders and operators of particular wet-cradle or defense facilities;
- the legal status of any autonomous open forms housed there;
- the facilities and operators responsible for the Three-Hand Rescue;
- and whether “partner world” is a political program, common usage, or only an
  analytical description.

No confirmed Manifold rupture, hostile Closed Night, or weaponized Agreement
deployment is assigned to Avara.
