# Valec

## Setting role

Valec is a densely inhabited industrial world destroyed in the only universally
accepted planetary Rzez deployment. Its destruction defines Year Zero of the
Compact Calendar and causes the formation of the Sixth Compact of Kharos.

Valec occupies a star system separate from Kharos. The world and its approach
routes are quarantined. Other bodies, habitats, and surviving stations in the
system remain undefined.

## Pre-destruction characteristics

The world has approximately 6.2 billion inhabitants and extensive deep-crust
infrastructure. Standardized materials, globally connected cities, and unusually
continuous crystalline formations make Valec efficient and especially legible to
Rzez characterization.

That efficiency gave ordinary households a reason to trust the same systems
that later carried failure. Districts shared water, transport, precision parts,
repair standards, and deep service structures. Standardization was a civil
achievement before it became a targeting advantage. Valec must not be written
as a population knowingly choosing one planetary vulnerability after the
Compact already taught its lesson.

## Destruction

The attack develops from ordinary-looking failures into the four-month Gray
Season, continental descent, and final loss of crustal and mantle continuity.
Recognizable geography disappears over eleven days. Fewer than three million
people escape.

The operator remains officially unresolved. Several states possess partial
capability at the time.

## Why warning did not become escape

The Gray Season was not four months of agreed planetary evacuation. Officials
attributed early failures to defects and geological stress, suppressed attack
claims, and continued to deny an array whose emitters had been disguised as
monitoring equipment over decades. The useful warning available to a later
reader was fragmented among frightened residents, failing instruments, and
authorities demanding an emitter before accepting the diagnosis.

Nor could recognition instantly provide lift and receiving capacity for 6.2
billion people. Every departure needed a functioning path to a launch site,
sound pressure and structural systems, propulsion, space to receive the
passengers, and continued life support after arrival. The same material failure
was degrading those paths. Many craft broke during ascent or docking; others
failed later after physical cargo or possessions transmitted target inheritance.
Reaching orbit was not always survival.

The fewer-than-three-million escape figure refers to people who survived
departure from Valec, not to a verified count of people who immediately reached
another star system. The distribution among nearby refuge, onward passage, and
later diaspora remains unassigned. Any later account specifying it must supply
transport and shelter capacity rather than granting the catastrophe an
unlimited emergency fleet.

Surviving departure packets carry variants of **leave what knew the ground**:
abandon surface-exposed cargo before meeting uncontaminated vessels or habitats.
The instruction concerned material custody. Voices, testimony, and remotely
received records did not carry a Rzez field. Present survivor disputes concern
how precautions were applied, who was refused, and how many material witnesses
were lost with the possessions; the physics does not make every Valeci person
or descendant a permanent contagion.

## Persistent condition

Twenty-three years later, surviving matter remains chemically ordinary but
cannot reliably form large, persistent structures. Local stone rejects durable
attachment; imported material can acquire the same pattern. Importing Valec
fragments is therefore absolutely prohibited by the Compact.

Valec retains mass and gravity. Loss of durable shear-bearing attachment does
not prevent rubble, fluids, or pressure-supported matter from collecting into
a broadly spherical gravitational body. The disaster destroyed a coherent,
habitable world and the capacity of sampled material to build enduring
structures; it did not make a planet's worth of matter refuse gravitational
attraction. Later writing should distinguish changing topography, settling,
and orbital debris from material escaping the system entirely.

The survey at Compact Year +23 is a dated observation, not an uninterrupted
measurement extending to the present recension. Its prohibition survives
because successful restoration and safe sample transfer have not been
established. An unanswered present-condition question must not silently become
either a recovered world or a uniform rate of continuing decay.

## Cultural consequences

Valeci survivor communities influence architecture, mourning, language, and
continuity theory. Their cohesion ledgers record relationships as well as names.
They reject doctrines that deny ordinary personhood or funerary status to Rzez
victims.

Those ledgers also preserve obligations: a dependent whose provider vanished,
a household divided between vessels, an apprenticeship without its workshop,
and a death for which no body can be returned. A missing physical grave does not
erase an inheritance or settle a contested account. Survivor institutions keep
material quarantine separate from the legal standing of people, memories, and
authenticated copies whose carriers have a clean history.

The diaspora is not one political voice. Refugees experienced different
evacuation decisions and receiving jurisdictions; later descendants inherit
that disagreement as well as grief. Some seek access for better surveys, some
oppose any approach, and some regard the work of building a durable household
elsewhere as the only restoration the dead can presently receive. None of those
positions requires the Orchard Ship rumor to be true.

Their relationship to the [Blackglass Compendium](../../frame/blackglass-tradition.md)
is bitterly ambivalent. Blackglass preserves testimony that governments and
militaries separated or denied, but it also turns the destruction of a world
into a technical lineage. Valeci cohesion ledgers may quote a witness while
omitting filing numbers, synchronization values, and every constructive
cross-reference. Many memorial readers speak the names of the dead but refuse to
speak the complete name of the weapon.

Valeci religious and survivor authorities reject the claim that refusal to read
is ignorance. In their account, the unread parameter is sometimes the last
boundary the dead can still enforce. They are among the strongest opponents of
cults that call Valec a revelation, liberation, completed page, or proof that
Blackglass rewards comprehension.

## Unresolved questions

- Who deployed the planetary array?
- Is Valec still decaying?
- Does the site, the material, or an informational pattern carry contamination?
- Can any large-scale ecosystem or civilization be restored there?
- Is the rumored Orchard Ship real?
- Which Valeci Blackglass fragments preserve suppressed testimony, and which
  were planted later to claim authority over the dead?
