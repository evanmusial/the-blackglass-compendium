# Spaceflight, Economy, and Settlement

## Status and purpose

This document is the established authorial outline of how humanity explores
space, moves between worlds, opens ligatures, finances travel, founds settlements,
and remains divided across a small interstellar region.

It is an operating model rather than a list of scenic possibilities. Future
entries may create local exceptions, but they must pay the physical, demographic,
ecological, financial, and political costs described here.

The central decision is:

> Space is not empty distance conquered by a better engine. It is an economy of
> gravity wells, heat paths, reaction mass, closed carriages, reciprocal weight,
> delayed evidence, and communities that must learn to survive a severed route.

## Governing decisions

1. Ordinary ships use reaction mass. Ligatures do not replace surface launch,
   orbital transfer, or in-system propulsion.
2. A present route is a reciprocal locality exchange between two constructed
   shores. No one-sided gate or shipboard drive exists.
3. Every arrival exchanges with a matched departure. Interstellar traffic must
   balance invariant mass-energy even when value and population flow do not.
4. An otherwise unreachable new shore is opened by a slow uncrewed precursor
   mission, not by a heroic first crew jumping through an unexplored route.
5. Exploration, occupation, settlement, and a rooted society are different
   achievements.
6. Bulk matter is normally sourced inside a stellar system. Interstellar trade
   concentrates on people, biological diversity, authenticated information,
   precision goods, rare feedstocks, and emergency supply.
7. A colony is a relationship of dependency, not a synonym for any offworld
   community.
8. A world may contain millions and remain materially dependent. A smaller
   habitat network may be politically and industrially rooted.
9. Route access does not make evacuation easy. A world can die faster than its
   shores can exchange its population.
10. The known region remains eight to twelve inhabited systems because each
    additional society costs generations of precursor work, industrial mass,
    ecological care, migration, and political commitment.

## Working vocabulary

- **Expedition:** a temporary mission whose planned end is departure, relief, or
  replacement.
- **Outpost:** a continuously staffed facility without the population or
  industrial depth required for multigenerational life.
- **Dependent settlement:** a permanent community with locally born residents
  that would fail after prolonged route loss.
- **Continuant settlement:** a multigenerational community able to preserve life,
  reproduction, education, and government through a serious interruption.
- **Rooted polity:** a society able to replace its critical systems and train its
  own successors. Trade enriches it; route loss does not immediately end it.
- **Colony:** a settlement whose sponsor retains decisive political or economic
  authority. The word describes power, not distance from Earth or Kharos.
- **Foundation:** the deliberate creation of a permanent settlement. The term
  does not claim that the destination was empty, harmless, or morally available.
- **Shore:** the permitted ligature endpoint volume within a stellar system.
- **Weighhouse:** the distributed infrastructure that prepares, exchanges, and
  localizes route carriages.
- **Carriage:** the closed mass-energy packet exchanged through a ligature.
- **Weight right:** a contractual claim upon part of a scheduled carriage class.
- **Mailfall:** the localization and opening of a carriage carrying accumulated
  messages, records, markets, and orders from another system.
- **Route-loss horizon:** the time before a settlement loses an indispensable
  life, medical, industrial, or governmental function after traffic stops.
- **Replacement depth:** how many generations of tools and components a local
  industry can reproduce before encountering an imported dependency.

## Exploration status

The known world distinguishes discovery from possession and a visit from a
society.

### Detected

A star, body, atmosphere, signal, or conjugacy candidate appears in remote data.
Detection does not establish composition, accessibility, life, artificiality, or
safe approach.

### Characterized

Independent observations constrain orbit, mass, radiation, atmosphere, major
materials, or locality coupling well enough to test a mission proposal. A
characterized planet may never receive a probe.

### Visited

An instrument physically enters the system or reaches the body. One flyby, failed
receiver seed, or uncontrolled arrival counts as a visit and nothing more.

### Instrumented

Several independent sensors persist long enough to establish local time,
navigation, weather, radiation, and environmental change. An instrumented system
may remain uninhabited and lack a ligature shore.

### Sampled

Material or biological evidence is collected under a documented physical custody
chain. Remote spectra, one dependent sample, and a returned specimen are not
interchangeable evidence.

### Sustained exploration

The system supports repeated missions, long-baseline observation, maintained
archives, and meaningful search of more than one convenient target. The canonical
claim that every substantially explored system contains a Blackglass fragment or
parallel correspondence applies at this stage, not to every detected star.

### Opened

A reciprocal ligature has passed independent certification and both shores can
support traffic, refusal, rescue, and quarantine. Opening creates access; it does
not establish a right to settle.

### Continuously inhabited

A continuant settlement has locally born generations, independent life and civic
systems, and a serious route-loss horizon. Expeditions, mines, survey camps, and
automated shores do not enter the inhabited-system count.

No probe, flag, shore patent, or first Answer grants ownership of a biosphere or
political authority over later residents. Jurisdictions disagree about resource
claims and opening concessions, but the distinction between evidence, access,
and government is established.

## The physical geography of travel

### Surface, orbit, system, shore, route

One interstellar journey contains several different transport economies:

`surface -> local orbit -> system transfer -> shore service -> ligature passage -> shore service -> system transfer -> destination orbit or surface`

A route carriage does not land on a planet. A surface lander does not normally
cross a ligature. A freighter optimized for months of electric propulsion is not
automatically a safe passenger carriage. Tankers, tenders, tugs, ferries, route
carriers, and landers remain separate assets because each performs a different
physical job.

Door-to-door travel can therefore take much longer than route depth. A passenger
may spend days climbing from Kharos and clearing orbital medicine, weeks reaching
and waiting at the shore, days inside a strong ligature, then weeks crossing the
destination system and quarantine.

### Gravity wells decide what moves

Moving common matter upward from a large inhabited world is usually irrational
when airless moons, asteroids, and orbital industry can provide it more cheaply.

- High-gravity worlds export people, institutions, designs, specialized living
  products, and high-value manufacture more readily than rock or water.
- Low-gravity bodies supply propellant, shielding, bulk structure, ballast, and
  construction mass.
- Atmospheres support aerobraking and local industry while making clean vacuum
  launch harder.
- Route shores favor dynamically quiet, sparsely inhabited regions with room for
  arrival error, momentum works, ballast yards, and quarantine.

The route city is therefore usually an orbital and deep-space complex rather than
the capital below it.

## Three paths to a shore

### Frontier opening

The conjugate system has no other usable access. A receiver seed must cross
ordinary interstellar distance and bootstrap the far weighhouse. This is the
slowest, most expensive, and most historically consequential path. The staged
sequence below describes frontier opening.

### Cross-link opening

The far system is already reachable through another ligature. Crews, clocks,
ballast, and weighhouse machinery can arrive through the existing network and
construct the new shore locally. Cross-links can be completed within years or
decades, but they depend upon an already inhabited or heavily instrumented system
and may alter the strategic balance among several routes.

### Reopening

An abandoned, inherited, or orphaned weighhouse already occupies the shore.
Surveyors must authenticate its locality tables, material condition, control
history, old schedules, debts, hazards, and consent before reuse. Reopening may be
faster than new construction or slower than starting over. A physically valid old
shore does not carry its former government's authority with it.

## Frontier opening sequence

### Stage 1: remote census

Telescopes, interferometers, stellar seismology, planetary spectra, gravitational
survey, and long-baseline locality experiments identify a candidate conjugate
system. The survey estimates:

- stellar and planetary architecture;
- volatile, radiation, and debris environments;
- possible native atmospheres or biospheres;
- the likely far-shore volume;
- ligature coupling and expected depth;
- ordinary sublight travel and braking requirements; and
- whether any economic or scientific reason justifies proceeding.

Most candidates end here. A measurable conjugacy may be too deep, too unstable,
too hazardous, or attached to a system no sponsor will spend generations opening.

### Stage 2: receiver-seed mission

A **receiver seed** is an uncrewed interstellar foundation craft. It travels
through ordinary space by beamed sail, fusion stage, or a staged combination,
normally over decades to centuries.

Every mission must close the braking account before launch. Accepted architectures
include a carried fusion stage, magnetic or electric sail, photonic braking,
staged disposable braking mass, or a deliberately slow approach. A fast seed
without destination capture is classified as a flyby, not an opening mission.

The seed is not a universal self-replicator. It carries:

- several independent power sources;
- redundant navigation and stellar-capture systems;
- machine tools and certified precision parts;
- prospecting, mining, refining, and additive/subtractive fabrication equipment;
- clocks, reference masses, and locality instruments;
- fault-isolated control systems;
- enough stored feedstock to repair the tools that begin local production; and
- multiple plans for constructing a weighhouse from different destination
  materials.

Its machines can bootstrap a bounded industrial chain. They cannot make every
semiconductor, catalyst, superconductor, clock, or biological system from raw
stone. The seed must arrive with the irreplaceable high-order components needed
to build the first return.

### The near-shore vigil

The sponsor must construct the near experimental weighhouse before the seed could
possibly answer and preserve it through the long flight. A vigil maintains
sequence clocks, recurring Answer windows, test countercarriages, project
archives, and legal succession. Its staff may serve a machine launched by their
grandparents or by a state that no longer exists.

If the shared Answer calendar is lost, the seed may transmit an ordinary-light
readiness notice after arrival and wait for the response. The delay can add
decades. A vigil that ends too early can create a technically complete orphan
shore.

### Stage 3: capture and shore search

The precursor brakes into the destination basin and searches the predicted shore
family. A successful astronomical solution may still miss the engineering volume
by millions of kilometres. The seed deploys clocks, ranging stations, reference
masses, and prospectors before committing its construction inventory.

A failed seed may pass the system, exhaust its braking reserve, choose unusable
feedstock, or build at a locality node too weak for a safe carriage. Unless it can
send ordinary light home, its sponsors may learn nothing for decades.

### Stage 4: far-shore construction

The seed establishes power, ballast, heat rejection, momentum capacity, isolated
carriage volumes, and at least two independent control histories. This normally
takes ten to forty years after arrival. A single operating rig may attempt a
microscopic exchange; it is not yet a public shore.

Before launch, the seed and home shore receive the same sequence clocks and a
recurring table of Answer windows beginning after the earliest plausible arrival
and bootstrap. The home shore must preserve a small countercarriage and successor
institution for those attempts, sometimes across centuries. A far seed that
misses every surviving window may finish a technically sound orphan shore with no
recognized counterparty.

### Stage 5: the Answer

The first reciprocal exchange returns destination matter, instrument records,
and a physical proof that the far weighhouse exists. It is traditionally called
**the Answer**.

One Answer proves an incident. It does not prove a safe route. Fraud, dependent
clocks, contaminated models, and one unstable coupling remain possible.

### Stage 6: independent proof

A ligature is recognized as navigable only after at least three independently
instrumented reciprocal passages using different clocks, control histories,
reference masses, and carriage hardware. Each shore must demonstrate full-scale
precommitment refusal and autonomous completion, controlled subscale snapback,
quarantine, and localization without relying upon one remote authority. Crew- or
freight-class carriages are not deliberately stressed merely to improve an
evidence grade.

The compact formulation is:

> First passage is an incident. First Answer is a hypothesis. Independent return
> makes a route.

### Stage 7: destination watch

Before people cross, the far system receives and constructs:

- rescue and quarantine capacity;
- a larger ballast reserve;
- independent power and heat paths;
- system navigation and planetary survey;
- several years of autonomous stores;
- shelters outside the weighhouse;
- ordinary local spacecraft; and
- an authenticated archive that does not depend upon the founding seed's one
  control history.

### Stage 8: provisional opening

Crews, scientists, and construction specialists arrive in small carriages. They
remain an expedition until they can survive a missed return, repair the far shore,
and depart through at least two independently controlled carriage sets. This does
not require a second ligature.

### Stage 9: public opening

A route is publicly open when both shores possess independent traffic control,
rescue, quarantine, localization capacity, audited mass books, and at least two
unrelated operators able to refuse unsafe traffic.

The physical ligature belongs to neither operator. The shore infrastructure and
its service concession may be privately, civically, religiously, militarily, or
cooperatively owned.

## Exploration timescales

The following working authorial ranges describe a **frontier opening**:

- candidate survey and conjugacy confirmation: **5–25 years**;
- receiver-seed design and launch: **5–20 years**;
- ordinary interstellar precursor flight: normally **50–300 years**, depending
  upon distance, velocity, and braking; roughly thirty years is reserved for an
  unusually near target and an approximately `0.15c`-class mission with a closed
  capture plan;
- far-shore bootstrap: **10–40 years**;
- independent route proof: **5–20 years**;
- provisional human opening: usually **one to several centuries** after the
  candidate became serious;
- industrial outpost after opening: **10–25 years**;
- continuant settlement: normally **50–100 years**;
- rooted polity: normally **100–250 years**;
- mature system: generally **several centuries**.

A cross-link inside an already accessible system may proceed from survey to
public opening in roughly **5–30 years**. Reopening ranges from a few years for a
well-preserved audited shore to longer than new construction when old control,
hazard, ownership, or locality records cannot be trusted.

These are not waiting periods that one rich state can purchase away. Stars must
be crossed, machinery must operate, ecosystems must cycle, children must become
replacement specialists, and institutions must survive their founders.

Long human lives allow some individuals to see more than one stage. They do not
turn expansion into one career.

fabrication, constitutional records, and route repair travel in physically and
administratively independent carriages.

## Route operations and capacity

### Cold reception

Every arrival first localizes into an isolated, sacrificial reception cell
physically separated from the principal weighhouse and inhabited traffic. The
carriage remains closed and mechanically cold until:

- exterior mass, charge, heat, radiation, and motion checks close;
- remote samplers authenticate atmosphere and exposed material where applicable;
- active energy stores and actuators are bounded;
- the manifest is compared with independent shore records; and
- local quarantine authorizes opening or transfer to a more distant pad.

A compromised far shore can still sacrifice a reception cell or attempt to hide
dangerous cargo. It cannot casually place one scheduled carriage inside the route
city's inhabited pressure or central control volume.

### Standard weight classes

Each ligature supports a small family of certified carriage harmonics. Ports
standardize those into **weight classes**. A passenger does not purchase the right
to send an arbitrary spacecraft; the passenger purchases volume and life support
inside a carriage whose total invariant mass is already fixed.

Unused capacity is filled with trim mass or useful ballast. Adding one person
normally displaces water, shielding, or freight rather than changing the route's
total weight.

Different classes exist for letters, passengers, common freight, cold cargo,
hazards, and foundation mass. A strong core ligature may sustain several classes
or modes simultaneously. A frontier ligature may exchange one common carriage
every few weeks.

### Throughput

Working ranges, not universal specifications:

- an experimental shore conducts a few exchanges per year;
- a frontier route conducts roughly a dozen to several dozen major exchanges per
  year;
- a mature secondary route conducts weekly or near-daily exchanges;
- a core trunk with several modes may conduct hundreds of major exchanges per
  year.

Annual traffic ranges from thousands of tonnes on a fragile frontier route to one
or several million tonnes of **gross exchanged mass**, summed across both
directions, on the strongest mature trunks. Useful freight is lower because gross
mass includes carriage structure, thermal stores, life support, trim, and
counterweight. Passenger capacity is normally thousands to hundreds of thousands
per year, not billions.

These values allow migration, specialist exchange, and meaningful trade while
making planetary evacuation physically impossible on the timescale of an attack.

### One worked core-trunk year

The following is an authoring example, not a universal carriage standard.

A mature ligature has:

- route depth of eight days;
- four active harmonics;
- roughly thirty-six hours of recovery after each exchange;
- and 144 scheduled paired exchanges in one local year after maintenance margin.

Its schedule contains:

| Class | Paired cycles | Gross mass at each shore | Annual gross mass, both directions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Letter | 24 | 50 tonnes | 2,400 tonnes |
| Passenger | 64 | 2,000 tonnes | 256,000 tonnes |
| Common freight | 40 | 10,000 tonnes | 800,000 tonnes |
| Foundation | 16 | 25,000 tonnes | 800,000 tonnes |

The total is approximately **1.86 million tonnes of gross exchanged mass**. If
common and foundation weights average roughly sixty percent useful payload, the
route moves about 900,000 tonnes of useful freight across both directions, plus
mail and approximately 38,000 passengers at 300 people per passenger carriage.

The passenger mass is dominated by survival rather than seating. At an austere
one kilowatt of metabolic and life-support heat per person, 300 travelers produce
about 207 gigajoules during eight days. Storing that burden only as latent heat of
ice would require roughly 620 tonnes of ice. A real carriage combines cold mass,
phase stores, chemical sinks, regenerative systems, structure, shielding, trim,
water, food, and reserve. A deeper route carries fewer people in the same class or
devotes still more weight to heat.

The example fixes the accounting convention: **gross** includes both complete
carriages and ballast in both directions; **useful freight** excludes carriage
structure, service mass, and ballast moved only to close the route.

### Scheduling and mailfall

Both shores prepare the same crossing schedule in advance. No live handshake
exists. A carriage that fails to localize cannot report progress, and a shore that
dies may remain undiagnosed until a scheduled exchange is missed.

Commercial, legal, and personal messages accumulate between cycles. Their
arrival produces a **mailfall**: many communities receive weeks of news, prices,
judgments, deaths, births, and orders at once. Markets and governments do not
share one continuously updated interstellar present.

### Route stillness

Carriages lock tanks, cargo, rotating equipment, weapons, and large adaptive
structures before commitment. Passenger motion is permitted within a compensated
interior, but every moving mass consumes trim authority. High-motion cargo, large
animals, unstable fluids, and active machinery pay a higher service cost because
they increase chatter and spill risk.

## The physical cost of passage

Fuel is only one part of price. The route cost stack includes:

1. surface and orbital lift;
2. in-system transfer to the shore;
3. carriage preparation and inspection;
4. certified weight-class capacity;
5. counterweight acquisition at the other shore;
6. sequence-mode occupancy;
7. endpoint energy and heat rejection;
8. local momentum and orbital reconciliation;
9. life support or thermal service;
10. rescue and quarantine reserves;
11. insurance and political risk;
12. time while capital remains inaccessible; and
13. onward transport after arrival.

Cargo is priced along at least six physical axes:

- invariant mass;
- volume;
- internal motion and multipole complexity;
- heat generated or requiring removal;
- biological, radiological, chemical, informational, or arcane hazard;
- and urgency.

A tiny living culture may cost more than tonnes of inert metal because it needs
power, custody, atmosphere, thermal control, and quarantine for the entire
journey.

## Reciprocity and the ballast economy

### Trade balances in mass, not value

Every useful arrival needs an equal outbound carriage at the destination. A
system importing medicine, people, or precision machinery must send equivalent
mass the other way even when it has no equally valuable export.

Water, regolith, slag, shielding, reaction feedstock, obsolete machinery, and
certified scrap therefore possess **route value** beyond their commodity value.
The opposite shore may need the material, or it may merely need a safe conserved
burden to complete the passage.

### Ballast is sovereignty

A settlement without accessible, characterized counterweight cannot freely shape
its traffic. It cannot receive net inbound relief or immigrants unless it sends
equivalent people, cargo, or other mass, and it cannot fill an outbound weight
class whose useful payload is too light. Wealth held as distant credit cannot
substitute for local mass in the weighhouse.

Evacuation remains possible when the evacuees and their vessels fill the outbound
class and the receiving shore supplies the matched return carriage. A local
ballast shortage reduces flexibility and throughput; it does not by itself make
every departure impossible.

Shore governments therefore maintain public ballast reserves alongside air,
water, fuel, medicine, and emergency power. Airless moons, asteroid yards, and
low-gravity stockpiles gain political importance because they can replenish the
mass book cheaply.

> A shore without weight is a door whose handle is on the other side.

### Counterflow and backhaul

Valuable trade can move predominantly in one direction while physical mass moves
both ways. Traders compete for the counterflow because a low-value return cargo
may travel cheaply inside a passage already justified by high-value imports.

This creates legitimate backhaul markets and abusive **waste colonialism**. A
powerful shore may attempt to balance its imports by sending contaminated slag,
obsolete machinery, toxic industry, prisoners, or politically unwanted people.
Counterweight composition is therefore subject to customs, consent, quarantine,
and environmental law rather than treated as anonymous mass.

### Momentum is also an account

Two carriages can match in invariant mass and differ dangerously in their local
orbital frames. Weighhouses use flywheels, mass rings, electromagnetic stores,
reaction mass, and controlled trajectories to reconcile the difference.

Each shore defines a standard route four-momentum in its own comoving frame. The
paired ligature state exchanges those normalized assignments, so a weighhouse
does not pay the full relative velocity of two stars or compare an absolute
potential between them. It pays the local work of bringing a departure from its
actual orbit to the standard and moving an arrival onward.

For scale, a 10,000-tonne common carriage with a residual local velocity of 100
metres per second carries about 50 gigajoules of kinetic mismatch and one billion
kilogram-metres per second of momentum. A mature yard can absorb that through
distributed machinery; it cannot ignore it. A full mass book can still conceal
an empty momentum reserve.

## What crosses between stars

### Principal trade

Interstellar traffic favors value that survives carriage cost:

- authenticated scientific, legal, historical, and commercial records;
- trained specialists, migrants, diplomats, pilgrims, students, and refugees;
- pharmaceuticals, catalysts, and specialized biological products;
- reproductive diversity, tissue lineages, crop varieties, microbes, and
  ecological recovery packages;
- precision optics, sensors, clocks, semiconductors, and quantum devices;
- certified superconducting elements, reactor components, control hardware, and
  machine tools;
- rare isotopes or catalysts unavailable locally;
- material standards with trusted provenance;
- art, relics, religious objects, and custody-bearing artifacts;
- route components that the destination cannot yet reproduce; and
- emergency medicine, food cultures, or famine relief.

### Normally local goods

- water;
- atmospheric gases;
- common structural metal;
- shielding mass;
- construction aggregate;
- ordinary food;
- bulk reactor fuel; and
- low-precision finished goods.

Shipping staple grain, air, or water between stars indicates disaster,
dependency, blockade, or luxury. A mature settlement finds common mass locally or
does not remain mature.

### Why finished goods still travel when designs are copyable

A design is not a factory. High-order manufacture depends upon tooling,
calibration, clean feedstock, process history, biological cultures, skilled
judgment, and trusted certification. A copied model can be cheap while the first
reliable local implementation remains the work of a generation.

One of the region's most valuable exports is therefore **trustworthy
independence**: a calibration standard, clean tissue lineage, separately gathered
record, or machine whose control and material histories can be audited.

## Finance and insurance

### Capital travels into an evidentiary delay

No regional market knows every current fact. A ship may have arrived, failed,
been quarantined, or been seized while investors elsewhere still trade the old
manifest.

Contracts therefore record:

- originating execution time;
- sequence window;
- destination localization time;
- custody and hazard state;
- and the earliest carriage expected to return authenticated receipt.

Payment commonly remains in escrow until destination evidence returns. Prices,
currencies, and credit conditions diverge between mailfalls. Interstellar
arbitrage carries route depth, schedule, and political risk rather than occurring
instantaneously.

### Route-opening finance

Opening a shore outlives ordinary commercial patience. Typical instruments
include:

- public or dynastic endowments;
- guild and university prospecting shares;
- religious or diaspora subscriptions;
- receiver-seed bonds;
- route-opening bonds payable after independent route proof;
- shore construction concessions;
- foundation bonds backed by future traffic fees; and
- mutual rescue and quarantine funds.

The first generation commonly pays for an Answer it will never see. Durable
institutions, not only bold individuals, open the region.

### Custodial succession and long debt

Receiver-seed claims reside in perpetual public trusts, university or guild
foundations, religious endowments, or transferable custodial registries. They do
not accrue ordinary compound interest for centuries. At a defined horizon,
interest converts to a capped opening concession, audited traffic share, or fixed
repayment schedule.

If the issuer disappears, claims pass through published succession and escheat
rules rather than expanding without limit. An orphan shore may recognize the
verified construction principal and a capped return without inheriting every
political promise or speculative derivative issued by a dead sponsor.

Foundation debt may not collateralize breathable air, basic water, reproductive
archives, emergency medicine, childhood, or the right to depart under the
dominant core legal practice. A resident charter can repudiate unlimited ancestral
interest without denying that real material was supplied.

### Insurance is reciprocal

The two exchanged carriages share one physical event. Each side therefore has a
material interest in the other's manifest, maintenance, internal motion, and
hazard classification. A dangerous countercarriage can injure both shores.

Coverage distinguishes:

- refusal;
- snapback;
- spill or shorefall;
- operational de-rating, forecast secular fade, or abrupt anomalous loss;
- momentum debt;
- ordinary vessel failure;
- quarantine;
- political seizure or blockade;
- biological contamination;
- and exposure to a prohibited system.

Independent clocks, mass books, hull records, power histories, and shore
witnesses matter more than repeated copies of one log. General-average rules
share the cost when cargo, ballast, atmosphere, coolant, or machinery is
sacrificed to save both carriages. During equalization, sacrifice means consuming,
heating, flooding, venting into an internal reserve, or destroying material while
retaining every product inside the closed carriage. Jettison is possible only
before commitment or after localization. Salvage never outranks rescue.

Private insurers rarely cover a first receiver seed. States, guilds, religions,
or prospecting syndicates absorb that loss. Commercial route insurance begins
only after independent reciprocal proof.

## Ownership, tolls, and shore power

### What can be owned

No one owns a conjugacy or the sequence field. Operators can own or govern:

- weighhouse stations;
- ballast and momentum reserves;
- clocks and calibration infrastructure;
- current phase and localization tables;
- carriage hulls;
- rescue, quarantine, and customs facilities;
- approach-volume security; and
- a service concession.

The old saying is:

> No one owns the ligature. Someone always owns the scales.

### Bilateral governance

A passage requires both shores. Major routes therefore develop bilateral shore
compacts covering:

- admissible weight classes;
- hazardous cargo;
- timetable and sequence tables;
- customs and personhood;
- rescue liability;
- quarantine refusal;
- military traffic;
- mail custody;
- and procedures after one jurisdiction collapses.

Neither shore can compel the other to exchange. A monopoly can nevertheless make
formal equality coercive when one settlement depends upon traffic for survival.

### Opening concessions and common carriage

Many jurisdictions grant the builders a time-limited opening concession or allow
audited repayment plus a capped return. Once the original investment is repaid,
core routes commonly become regulated common-carrier services. This is a regional
practice, not universal law.

Tolls pay for weight class, mode time, localization work, ballast service, thermal
burden, rescue, quarantine, and hazard liability. A **weight right** is a claim to
part of one scheduled carriage, not ownership of a path through nature.

### Weight clearinghouses

Every mature route has a bilateral clearing institution, sometimes shared across
a hub. It:

- matches opposite-direction carriage demand;
- prices scarce counterflow and unused weight;
- reserves public emergency ballast;
- distinguishes gross carriage mass from useful payload;
- tracks where route mass becomes dispersed into shielding, habitation, cargo,
  or local industry;
- carries forward shore-local imbalances between schedules; and
- settles financial payment separately from the physical mass book.

Ballast is not consumed by a successful exchange. It changes shore and may cease
to be available because it was sold, built into a habitat, contaminated, or moved
far from the weighhouse. Clearing is therefore a logistics problem as well as an
accounting one.

## Colonization and foundation

### Why people found new societies

No single motive explains settlement.

- access to a strategically placed shore;
- low-gravity industry or rare local feedstock;
- a habitable or scientifically unique environment;
- refuge from law, war, caste, debt, religion, or ecological loss;
- deliberate redundancy of humanity and managed Earth life;
- military positioning;
- university, monastic, or archival seclusion;
- profit from route service;
- forced relocation or dispossession; and
- the ordinary desire to build a life elsewhere.

Most destinations are not profitable in the first generations. A foundation is
a long capital sink whose early returns may be science, route option, strategic
depth, or human survival rather than dividends.

### Survey before settlement

A planet with air and water may be harder to settle than a barren moon. Native
chemistry, pathogens, toxins, incompatible ecology, gravity, radiation, weather,
and the moral cost of contamination must be studied before open release.

The normal sequence is:

1. orbital and robotic census;
2. stand-off sample return;
3. sealed surface or atmospheric stations;
4. multi-season biological and geological survey;
5. independent contamination review;
6. limited occupied habitats;
7. local industry and food trials;
8. resident decision about permanent foundation; and
9. only then, any controlled environmental integration.

Terraforming is never the opening move.

## The foundation package

A credible foundation program delivers a civilization seed rather than a crowd.

Minimum established practice includes:

- three physically separable power systems using at least two energy sources;
- three pressure and life-support districts with independent shutdown;
- years of atmosphere, water, food, medicine, and cold reserve;
- local extraction, refining, chemistry, and waste processing;
- machine tools and additive/subtractive fabrication;
- semiconductor, optical, clock, and control-system repair stocks;
- spare radiators, pressure hull, seals, and power conversion;
- landing, orbital transfer, rescue, and quarantine craft;
- medical, obstetric, pediatric, dental, and rehabilitation capacity;
- gamete, embryo, tissue, crop, microbial, and industrial-organism archives;
- several unrelated food and nutrient pathways;
- schools and technical apprenticeships;
- legal, linguistic, scientific, and cultural archives;
- locally authoritative clocks and navigation;
- contamination and native-life laboratories;
- at least one independent means of repairing the far shore; and
- duplicate constitutional records carried through unrelated control histories.

A serious foundation moves hundreds of thousands of tonnes over decades. No one
ship contains the only viable ecology, government, or future generation.

## The three settlement ledgers

Every permanent settlement keeps three distinct accounts.

### The material ledger

Power, heat rejection, water, atmosphere, food mass, feedstock, spare parts,
transport, and the replacement chain for the tools that make other tools.

### The living ledger

Human reproductive diversity, commensal organisms, crops, decomposers,
fermentation cultures, pollination or equivalent reproductive support,
micronutrient cycles, pathogens, and the ability to recover after losing one
species or facility.

### The civic ledger

Skills, education, language, memory, law, consent, child citizenship, emergency
authority, archives, and the independent institutions needed to disagree with a
route operator or sponsor.

A settlement can be rich in machinery and poor in teachers. It can possess ten
thousand embryos and no stable childhood. It can produce enough food while one
company owns every air valve. No one ledger substitutes for another.

## Demographic and industrial thresholds

The following are authoring ranges rather than universal in-world statutes.

### Expedition

- normally fewer than **1,000** residents;
- staffed for a mission;
- no assumption of permanent reproduction;
- evacuation or relief remains part of the operating plan; and
- does not count as an inhabited system.

### Dependent settlement

- normally at least **3,000** permanent residents;
- locally born children;
- reproductive archives representing tens of thousands of unrelated donors;
- several independent life-support districts;
- local water, food, and basic fabrication; but
- a route-loss horizon still measured in months or a few years.

### Continuant settlement

A system counts as continuously inhabited when it normally has:

- at least **10,000** permanent residents;
- several locally born age cohorts;
- three geographically and mechanically separable communities;
- unrelated power, food, and archive systems;
- locally trained clinicians, engineers, teachers, and operators;
- at least **five years** of survival under route closure; and
- no single imported archive or machine whose loss ends reproduction, atmosphere,
  food, or government.

### Rooted polity

Usually, though not invariably:

- population of roughly **100,000 or more**;
- local replacement of critical power, pressure, medical, agricultural, and
  transport systems;
- education of its own replacement specialists;
- a route-loss horizon of a decade or more;
- resident control of air, water, labor law, childhood, medicine, and policing;
- and the ability to negotiate with the shore as a government rather than a work
  camp.

Replacement means **functional survival**, not parity with the core. A rooted
polity may use robust fission after losing advanced fusion blankets, simpler
processors after losing quantum equipment, chemical synthesis in place of an
imported pharmaceutical, and slower drives instead of a licensed torch. Its
technology can descend gracefully without its population dying.

### Mature system

- multiple settlements, habitats, or inhabited bodies;
- population in the hundreds of thousands, millions, or more;
- several ecological, industrial, legal, and cultural traditions;
- more than one major power and food architecture; and
- enough replacement depth that route loss causes contraction and hardship
  rather than immediate civilizational failure.

Genetic archives reduce biological bottlenecks. They do not supply parents,
technicians, constituencies, friendships, languages, or political legitimacy.

## Ecology and biological custody

No foundation imports one simplified “Earth ecosystem.” It carries several
managed packages with recorded provenance and overlapping functions.

Required functions include:

- primary food production;
- decomposition;
- nitrogen and mineral cycling;
- fermentation and food processing;
- pollination or equivalent reproductive support;
- soil or substrate maintenance;
- microbial diversity;
- disease surveillance;
- and recovery after one organism, archive, or growth facility is lost.

No colony relies upon one crop, one microbial vat, one pollinator, one protein
source, or one atmosphere organism. Biological archives travel in unrelated cold
carriages and are revived in separate facilities.

Native life is not treated as free biomass. It may be toxic, fragile, legally
protected, scientifically irreplaceable, or simply unrelated to human nutrition.
Open ecological release requires a resident political decision after long survey,
not a sponsor's remote schedule.

## Governance through foundation

### Expedition mandate

Narrow command authority governs immediate navigation, quarantine, life support,
and rescue. It is justified by a mission with a planned end.

### Founding council

Permanent households and locally born children create interests no launch
contract represented. Residents acquire control over labor, education, medical
care, reproduction, habitat rules, and local resource allocation.

### Resident charter

Sponsor authority expires or narrows. The settlement governs air, water, heat,
medicine, policing, childhood, land use, and local taxation. Shore concessions
and industrial assets remain separately regulated.

### Rooted government

The polity can contract, tax, refuse traffic, regulate its shore, and negotiate
with other systems independently. It may remain culturally allied or economically
indebted without remaining a colony.

A company may own ships, docks, reactors, and a toll concession. It may not own
residents, condition breathable air upon political obedience, or pledge human
reproductive archives as collateral under the dominant core legal tradition.
Frontier violations of those principles are part of the setting, not evidence
that the principles do not exist.

## Dependency and exploitation

A settlement may be:

- legally independent but unable to replace its reactors;
- materially resilient but ruled by an external shore company;
- wealthy in route tolls and poor in food diversity;
- populous but dependent upon one medical culture;
- or politically subordinate while possessing the only ballast reserve that
  keeps its sponsor's route open.

The route-loss horizon must therefore list its shortest clocks separately:

- atmosphere;
- water;
- food and nutrients;
- heat rejection;
- medicine;
- reactor service;
- fabrication feedstock;
- skilled labor;
- reproductive diversity;
- shore repair; and
- civil legitimacy.

The shortest clock, not the average stockpile, defines the emergency.

## Failure and abandonment

### Route-opening failures

- false or commercially useless conjugacy;
- receiver seed lost in ordinary space;
- failed braking;
- incorrect stellar-wind, dust, or braking-medium estimates;
- century-scale drift in an autonomous seed's control goals or legal mandate;
- radiation and aging destroying irreplaceable high-order components;
- wrong shore volume;
- insufficient local feedstock;
- destination chemistry incompatible with the planned bootstrap process;
- capture, refusal, or repurposing by an already present population;
- bootstrap industry unable to reproduce one critical component;
- a home vigil ending before the first surviving Answer window;
- orphan shore whose sponsor no longer exists;
- independent Answers that do not agree;
- operational de-rating or forecast secular fade before investment recovery; and
- a valid destination whose ecology or politics makes settlement unacceptable.

### Settlement failures

- one reactor, pressure spine, fabricator, archive, or food culture becoming
  indispensable;
- demographic collapse or loss of replacement skills;
- reproductive archive corruption;
- incompatible imported ecologies;
- chronic micronutrient or microbial failure;
- insufficient radiators or heat sinks;
- native toxin or pathogen missed during survey;
- inability to make the tools needed to repair imported tools;
- sponsor control of atmosphere, medicine, or departure;
- route closure before local industry matures;
- emergency coordination converted into coercive Agreement architecture;
- continuous utilities creating Rzez propagation paths; and
- conflict while sponsor instructions remain weeks or months old.

Most settlements do not fail in one cinematic event. They postpone births,
cannibalize machinery, narrow diets, lose schools, abandon districts, sell weight
rights, and become an outpost again. Some survive physically while the society
their founders intended disappears.

### Abandonment

A withdrawing settlement must decide what remains powered, what biological
material may be removed, who owns the shore, what debts survive, and whether a
later salvage crew may reopen the site. An abandoned shore is not automatically
dead. An orphaned schedule can outlive its government.

## Salvage and quarantine

A derelict is not safe because its crew is dead. Initial recovery occurs at
stand-off pads or sacrificial orbits.

Salvage classifications remain separate:

- biological contamination;
- radiation;
- chemical or industrial hazard;
- suspect Rzez physical propagation;
- active Agreement control architecture;
- suspected Manifold incidence or anchor condition;
- Closed Night anchor hardware;
- strange-matter or Rehearsal residue;
- and restricted Blackglass construction knowledge.

Mechanism boundaries matter:

- Rzez risk follows exposed physical samples, carriers, tools, and continuous
  structures, not a received recording.
- Agreement testimony is inert, while live adaptive feedback architecture may
  reconstruct capability.
- A Manifold report is safe to read; a suspected active incidence is a spatial
  quarantine problem.
- Closed Night diagrams can be constructive knowledge while an anchor remains
  physical contraband.
- Apsis telemetry is evidence until combined with convergence, baryon-bias, and
  quench capability.

Salvage requires independent custody, material witnesses, and a cleanup bond.
Suspect wreckage is never towed directly into an inhabited yard for convenience.

Quarantine may refuse a ligature schedule. Because refusal can kill a dependent
settlement, it requires independent evidence, a stated hazard mechanism, periodic
review, and relief planning. A blockade disguised as quarantine is a recurring
political crime.

## War, blockade, and evacuation

### Shore warfare

A natural ligature cannot be conquered by firing through it.

- Weapons do not cross outside a committed carriage.
- A hostile arrival needs a participating or compromised opposite shore.
- Every military carriage sends equal mass in the other direction.
- A defender can safely refuse before commitment.
- Closing after commitment risks both carriages and both shores.
- A ship cannot divert between systems.
- Alternative weighhouses are immense, slow to build, and limited to known shore
  volumes.

War therefore targets weighhouses, clocks, ballast, tenders, momentum stores,
arrival volumes, and the local political authority willing to exchange.

Destroying one's own shore can deny an invasion and isolate one's own society.
Route denial is an act of severance, not a clean defensive switch.

### Blockade

A system with one practical ligature can be besieged. The blockader may control
the shore, refuse countercarriages, seize ballast, destroy tenders, or mine the
arrival volume. Emergency passage, neutral mail, family reunification, and
medical exchange are treaty questions rather than automatic physical rights.

### Evacuation

Evacuation requires:

- an operating local shore;
- a receiving shore willing to send equal counterweight;
- carriage slots;
- passenger life support and thermal capacity;
- local transport from the endangered world;
- destination housing and quarantine; and
- enough warning to repeat the exchange.

A core route moving hundreds of thousands of people per year cannot evacuate a
world of billions during a fast catastrophe. Leaders may save specialists,
children, archives, reproductive diversity, or political elites while most
people remain. Every choice creates a historical wound.

This is why interstellar civilization does not make a world-terminal weapon less
terminal.

## Kelan–Ruva's economic geography

### Kharos

Kharos supplies people, law, education, scholarship, medicine, finance, cultural
authority, complex services, and high-value manufacture. Its 1.54-g surface makes
bulk export costly. It imports offworld feedstock and exports things whose value
justifies lifting them.

Its route institutions issue contracts, custody findings, personhood decisions,
and quarantine orders. Kharos is not automatically where the shore itself is
built.

### Avara

Avara supplies low-gravity orbital assembly, large pressure structures,
atmospheric engineering, clinical preparation, ecological packages, recovery
facilities, passenger conditioning, and much of the system's route-carriage work.

Its managed atmosphere makes dependency visible. Avaran law treats reserves,
withdrawal of life support, and biological custody as political questions rather
than invisible utility management.

### Odran

Odran supplies vacuum processing, mass drivers, reference materials, clocks,
calorimetry, carriage calibration, quarantine pads, independent archives, and
certified reference ballast.

Its low gravity and airless geography make it a natural standards and custody
site. Bulk operational ballast and momentum works belong near the actual route
shore; Odran becomes a major mass yard only if the later regional map places a
shore conveniently.

### The wider system

Edras and inner platforms supply solar-intensive industry. Talran platforms work
with atmospheric feedstocks. The Serrin Belt and outer bodies supply water,
volatiles, shielding, reaction mass, and route ballast. Istra, the Ravel Reach,
and the Far Cloud provide cold storage, long baselines, and autonomous survey.

No one body is self-sufficient. Their different gravity, heat, material, and
light-time conditions create the home system's economy.

## Why only eight to twelve systems remain inhabited

The regional scale follows from cumulative filters:

1. Usable ligatures are rare.
   As an authoring constraint, roughly one potentially macroscopic conjugacy
   survives among every twenty to fifty stellar basins subjected to serious
   locality survey. Most rooted systems have no unopened candidate.
2. A candidate still requires a receiver seed to cross ordinary interstellar
   distance.
3. Many seeds fail or arrive after their sponsors disappear.
4. A far shore takes decades to bootstrap and independently prove.
5. Some valid ligatures are too deep or weak for useful traffic.
6. Many accessible systems justify science or extraction but not a permanent
   society.
7. A serious foundation consumes hundreds of thousands of tonnes, route slots,
   ecological diversity, specialists, and generations of subsidy.
8. New settlements compete with existing worlds for migrants and institutional
   attention.
9. Some systems become dead, prohibited, quarantined, blockaded, or disconnected.
10. A mine, observatory, or twelve-person station does not count as an inhabited
    system.
11. Route capacity prevents simultaneous unlimited expansion.
12. Post-Valec civilization distrusts societies whose survival depends upon one
    continuous supply path.

Humanity may have instrumented or briefly occupied several dozen systems while
maintaining multigenerational societies in only eight to twelve.

An inhabited system is therefore a historical achievement. Losing Valec cannot
be repaired by placing a flag on the next available planet.

## Deliberately unresolved

- The exact current count and names of inhabited systems beyond established
  places.
- Which present shores were first built by Meridian programs, inherited from an
  earlier human expansion, or reopened after abandonment.
- The fate of every receiver seed and orphan shore.
- Whether any modern candidate is conjugate with the Solar System.
- The founding carriers' transit mechanism.
- The total regional population.
- One universal currency, route authority, commercial code, or colonial law.
- Whether a nonhuman civilization has ever opened a ligature.
- The final political boundary between Kharos, the route institutions, and other
  core systems.

These unknowns preserve history and conflict. They do not weaken the physical or
economic model.

## Authoring checklist for a new place

Before adding an inhabited system, world, habitat, or shore, answer:

1. How was it first observed?
2. How did the receiver seed or prior shore arrive?
3. What does the ligature exchange, how deep is it, and what are its weight
   classes?
4. Where are the shore, ballast, momentum, rescue, and quarantine works?
5. How do ordinary ships reach the inhabited destination from the shore?
6. Which common mass is local, and which high-value dependency crosses stars?
7. Who financed opening, and who now owns the scales?
8. What is the shortest route-loss clock?
9. What material, living, and civic systems make the community continuant?
10. Is it an expedition, outpost, colony, continuant settlement, or rooted polity?
11. What native environment was altered, protected, or misunderstood?
12. What failed during foundation, and what institution remembers the cost?
13. What can blockade, quarantine, operational de-rating, secular fade, or
    anomalous route loss do to it?
14. Why does this place need to exist instead of reusing one already in canon?

A convincing settlement is not a population number beside a planet. It is a
chain of physical and human dependencies that has survived long enough to become
a history.
