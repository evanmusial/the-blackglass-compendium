# Canon and Engineering Principles

## Editorial principles

These principles are suitable for the Compendium as a whole.

### The Codex is a reconstruction

Entries synthesize technical fragments, records, testimony, material evidence,
law, scholarship, religious tradition, folklore, and deliberate falsehood. The
editorial voice may weigh evidence strongly without becoming omniscient.

### Uncertainty is structured

Contradiction should not be used as permission for arbitrariness. An entry names
the sources of uncertainty, assigns evidentiary weight where possible, and
distinguishes a disputed explanation from a disputed event.

### Metaphysical truth is not settled by historical confidence

A Grade A experiment can have an unexplained result. A Grade E myth can contain
an accurate technical detail. The evidence scale measures support for a claim,
not whether the universe is material, divine, informational, or something else.

### Technology produces a world around itself

A forbidden instrument should have industrial ancestors, engineering practice,
accidents, doctrine, countermeasures, law, language, trauma, ritual, and misuse.
The entry is a history of consequences rather than a catalog of powers.

### Costs and limits belong beside capabilities

A technology becomes more convincing when characterization, propagation,
failure modes, operator risks, imperfect detection, countermeasures, and delayed
consequences are treated as seriously as destructive scale.

### The Compendium preserves disagreements

If two traditions or schools disagree in a way that deepens the setting, canon
should preserve the disagreement. A later entry may add evidence without being
required to close it.

## Rzez-family engineering principles

The following are supported for the Rzez family. They are not yet universal laws
of every arcane technology.

### Structural identity

A stable object persists through organized relationships across time. The Rzez
calls this network a **cohesion architecture**. It includes material bonds,
interfaces, load paths, repair processes, and large-scale equilibria.

### Edge invalidation rather than node removal

Classical cohesion graph theory describes material units as nodes and their
load-bearing or identity-preserving relationships as edges. The Rzez primarily
invalidates edges. Surviving paths inherit more load, making failure nonlinear.

### Entropic bias rather than brute-force destruction

The Rzez makes disordered states easier for a target to enter. Gravity, pressure,
residual stress, thermal cycling, and ordinary use provide much of the eventual
destructive force.

### Characterization precedes efficient action

The instrument must establish a relationship with the target's structure.
Homogeneous or repeating systems are easier to characterize; heterogeneous,
changing, turbulent, discontinuous, or rapidly repairing systems are harder.

### Scale changes architecture

Large Rzez systems are not merely more powerful hand instruments. Surgical,
industrial, siege, strategic, and planetary uses require different emitter
arrangements, boundaries, transmission paths, and target knowledge.

### Damage can outlive the emitter

After cascade begins, a target can continue its own disassembly. New material
may inherit instability from damaged material, producing the feared residual
pattern called target inheritance or the butcher's stain.

## Candidate universal principles not yet adopted

The Rzez suggests, but does not establish for the whole setting, that:

- identity may be an engineerable property;
- the history of remaining together may contribute to present structure;
- observation or external records may stabilize continuity;
- institutions, laws, memories, and names may possess cohesion architectures;
- and arcane engineering may manipulate relationships or information more
  fundamentally than matter.

These ideas should remain candidates until other entries independently require
or contradict them.
