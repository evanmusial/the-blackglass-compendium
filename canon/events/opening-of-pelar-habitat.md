# The Opening of Pelar Habitat

## Chronological role

The Opening of Pelar Habitat occurs in Compact Year +188 at an offworld habitat
outside the Kelan–Ruva system. Its exact system and route position remain
withheld in the records available to the Blackglass recension. Pelar has 27,611
registered occupants when the attack begins.

It is the first accepted strategic autonomy-denial attack and the defining
atrocity of The Agreement.

## Supported sequence

Pelar's emergency coordination lattice successfully distributes control during
the opening attack. Conventional strikes breach three hull sectors, but local
crews seal seventy-three pressure boundaries within ninety seconds, stabilize
pressure, and preserve the inhabited sections. The civil system works as
designed.

The hostile lattice then enters through the same reciprocal emergency carrier.
It progressively increases the physiological and mechanical cost of independent
correction. Residents, door controllers, pressure systems, and rescue automation
do not receive one transmitted command. They converge upon the same locally
predictable action.

Four minutes and twelve seconds after the first release, every remaining powered
pressure door begins to open inside the same eight-tenths of a second. Residents
at manual boundaries begin their override strokes during that interval.
Different mechanisms finish later, but no registered habitat pressure door
remains closed.

The supported logs show that many participants retained private alarm and made
brief, unsuccessful divergent movements. The event is therefore not evidence of
a shared mind, unanimous belief, or voluntary mass death. It demonstrates
unanimity without union: separate people and machines remain present while the
capacity to sustain a different action is taken from them. The accepted count is
26,944 dead and 667 survivors, most protected by detached capsules, sealed
stores, repair craft, or independent suits outside the reciprocal carrier.

## Evidentiary significance

Pelar's surviving external records are globally consistent. The event is not a
Manifold rupture. Its defining anomaly is covariance: nominally independent
human and machine responses acquire matching latency and residual error because
they share a concealed active carrier.

Recordings made after the attack are inert. No responder becomes coupled by
viewing the event. The site does not establish interstellar propagation or a
signal that can cross ordinary route latency without local reciprocal
infrastructure.

## Shared consequence

Pelar changes emergency design across the known region. Later facilities retain:

- one-way shutdown paths;
- physically uncoupled pressure controls;
- asynchronous local clocks;
- protected variance keepers;
- and the ability to divide an emergency network into cells whose errors cannot
  be redistributed through one carrier.

These safeguards reduce ordinary coordination efficiency. That cost is the
surviving evidence that Pelar was understood.
