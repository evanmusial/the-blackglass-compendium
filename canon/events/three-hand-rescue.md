# The Three-Hand Rescue

## Chronological role

The Three-Hand Rescue occurs at an orbital pressure works above
[Avara](../places/avara.md) in Compact Year +164. It is the first accepted public
demonstration that a bounded reciprocal control lattice can redistribute
prediction error among several consenting operators without transferring
memory, merging identity, or surrendering final disconnection.

The rescue becomes the civil reference point from which The Agreement's later
weaponized lineage is reconstructed.

## Supported significance

At an Avaran orbital pressure works, a deformed bulkhead traps eleven workers
behind a narrowing seal while the surrounding frame continues to twist. Three
operators with incompatible control stations and sight lines coordinate the
load, the cut, and the extraction. The lattice returns a calibrated portion of
each operator's correction burden to the others, allowing the group to control
one unstable mechanical problem without destructive oscillation. All eleven
trapped workers survive.

The original architecture is deliberately narrow:

- participation is informed and limited to a small team;
- coupling ends through a hard physical disconnect;
- no participant receives another's thoughts or skills;
- one exterior observer and recorder remain outside the reciprocal path;
- and the operators retain measurable independent error throughout the event.

Those limits are not later ethical additions. They are part of the architecture
that made the rescue survivable.

## Historical misuse

Military programs cite the rescue as proof that convergence improves performance
while omitting its consent, boundedness, exterior variance, and disconnection
requirements. The weaponized family does not discover a new principle. It makes
continued participation compulsory, increases the redistributed burden, and
suppresses the independent error the rescue design preserved.

The distinction becomes central to the Articles of Bodily Dissent: coordination
is not Agreement exposure merely because several people act together.
