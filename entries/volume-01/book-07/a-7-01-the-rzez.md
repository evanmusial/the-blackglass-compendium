# The Rzez

**Canonical designation:** Directed Cohesion Reduction Instrument  
**Common translation:** *The Butcher*  
**Older names and regional forms:** Rzezh, Rjeś, Rezhek, the Quiet Knife, the Stone-Skinner, the Last Wedge  
**Engineering class:** Entropic, propagative, structure-selective  
**Military classification:** Proscribed world-terminal technology  
**Original industrial classification:** Precision mineral-separation apparatus  
**Known operational scales:** Surgical, industrial, siege, strategic, continental, planetary  
**Present legal status:** Manufacture, possession, simulation, reconstruction, or instruction prohibited under the Sixth Compact of Kharos  
**Confirmed surviving examples:** None accepted by the Compact Commission  
**Alleged surviving examples:** Between two and eleven, depending upon the source  
**Confirmed planetary deployment:** Valec  
**Suspected planetary or lunar deployments:** Three  
**Confirmed false alarms and political fabrications:** Seventeen  
**Primary associated figure:** Varek Ilyr (a quarry engineer of the First Quarry Houses, traditionally credited with the first controlled cohesion field)

> *The Rzez does not break matter. It breaks the agreement by which matter remains a thing.*

— Commentary attributed to Engineer-Magistrate Olan Vey (a later legal scholar of the Kharosi reconstruction period), date uncertain

## Editorial note on names, places, and sources

This entry is presented as a standalone reconstruction from technical fragments, military records, religious narratives, court proceedings, survivor testimony, and later scholarship. Parenthetical clarifications are included where a place, person, institution, or historical period first appears.

The historical record is not uniform. The Rzez existed for centuries under conditions of secrecy, military censorship, religious prohibition, and deliberate falsification. Some incidents are supported by physical evidence and multiple independent records. Others survive only as songs, trial testimony, confiscated correspondence, or regional folklore. To distinguish these categories, this entry uses the following evidentiary scale:

- **Grade A:** Physical remains, independent technical records, and credible contemporary testimony agree.
- **Grade B:** Strong documentary evidence exists, but material evidence is incomplete or inaccessible.
- **Grade C:** Multiple traditions agree on the central event, though important details conflict.
- **Grade D:** A single substantial source or a cluster of dependent sources supports the claim.
- **Grade E:** Folklore, rumor, propaganda, visionary testimony, or technically unexplained anomaly.

The scale assesses historical support, not metaphysical truth. Several Grade E traditions describe effects that later appeared in well-documented incidents.

## Abstract

The Rzez is the archetypal instrument of directed structural disassembly. It does not burn, crush, melt, irradiate, vaporize, or conventionally sever its target. It acts instead upon the relationships that allow matter to preserve internal coherence over time.

Under the Rzez field, solids progressively lose the capacity to distribute stress, maintain molecular and crystalline organization, or remain continuous across their own boundaries. The target may retain its mass, composition, appearance, and even temporary function after its cohesion architecture has already entered irreversible decline.

A wall exposed to the Rzez may stand for hours. Its mortar nevertheless ceases to distribute load correctly. Its stone separates along mineral boundaries. Its reinforcing metal delaminates at microscopic interfaces. Thermal expansion, vibration, gravity, or the pressure of a human hand eventually supplies the trivial force needed to complete the collapse.

At small scale, the Rzez is a precision tool. At military scale, it is a weapon of delayed structural uncertainty. At planetary scale, it is geological butchery.

The world is not exploded. Its mountains become aggregate. Its crust loses continuity. Its mantle separates into unstable mineral domains. Its oceans invade newly permeable rock. Its cities fail before their inhabitants understand that an attack has begun.

The Rzez is feared not merely because it destroys. It permits a structure to continue appearing whole after it has already been condemned.

## The name

The term *Rzez* is usually translated as “the Butcher,” although surviving philologists dispute whether that meaning is original.

The oldest Quarry House inscriptions use a three-sign root associated with separation, slaughter, apportionment, and the division of sacrificial animals after ritual killing. Later military dialects narrowed the word toward organized killing. In still later legal texts, *rzez* became a verb meaning “to render incapable of remaining whole.”

The name therefore contains several meanings at once:

- one who divides flesh,
- one who separates a whole into usable parts,
- one who kills methodically,
- one who leaves no intact body,
- and one who destroys the conditions under which repair remains possible.

The formal engineering academies initially rejected the translation “Butcher.” Their preferred term was “Separator.” That word disappeared from respectable technical use after the destruction of Valec (a densely inhabited industrial world destroyed during the late Meridian Age).

Among quarry workers, an older saying survived:

> *A hammer asks the stone to move. A wedge asks it to divide. The Rzez asks whether there was ever a stone at all.*

The name is also surrounded by ritual avoidance. In several successor cultures, engineers refer to it only by classification number. Valeci survivors often omit the final consonant, claiming that the complete word “closes the cut.” Kharosi military law forbids speaking the name inside an active geological station, a rule officially described as superstition and nevertheless enforced without exception.

## What the Rzez is

The Rzez is not a single artifact. It is a family of technologies descended from the discovery that structural continuity can be manipulated independently of mass, temperature, and conventional mechanical force.

The most famous Rzez instruments resembled blades, but the blade shape was neither necessary nor mechanically functional. It provided a stable focusing geometry, a familiar form for operators, and a politically useful fiction: rulers could describe the device as an unusually effective sword rather than admit that they possessed a general method for invalidating material cohesion.

The term eventually came to encompass five related things:

1. **The Rzez principle:** the theoretical manipulation of cohesion architecture.
2. **A Rzez field:** a localized entropic bias that weakens selected structural relationships.
3. **A Rzez instrument:** a device that generates and shapes the field.
4. **A Rzez event:** any uncontrolled or weaponized cascade of cohesion loss.
5. **The Rzez:** the legendary total technology, including the disputed planetary implementation sometimes called the World Butcher.

This ambiguity is important. Many old records claim that “the Rzez” was present when they mean only that some derivative field was used. Conversely, several states denied possessing the weapon by admitting only to “industrial cohesion equipment.”

## Technical foundations

### Structural identity

The Codex treats every stable object as more than an accumulation of matter.

An object persists because its components maintain relationships across time. Atoms remain bonded. Crystal grains transmit force. fibers retain alignment. Cells preserve membranes. Materials maintain distinctions between interior and exterior. A bridge persists because its members share load. A bone persists because its mineral and organic matrices remain interdependent. A planet persists because crust, mantle, oceans, atmosphere, and gravity participate in a continuing structural equilibrium.

These relationships form what entropic engineers call the **cohesion architecture** of an object.

A sword is therefore not merely iron. It is iron arranged into grains, phases, boundaries, surfaces, residual stresses, and repeating continuities that permit it to behave as one sword.

The Rzez attacks this architecture.

### Cohesion graph theory

Classical Codex notation models a structure as a graph:

- material units are nodes,
- bonds and load-bearing relationships are edges,
- continuity is represented by paths through the graph,
- redundancy is represented by alternative paths,
- structural integrity depends upon connectivity and the capacity to redistribute stress.

The Rzez does not primarily remove nodes. It progressively invalidates edges.

At low exposure, the target develops microscopic discontinuities. These may be too small to observe. As more connections fail, surviving connections carry greater load. The increased load accelerates further failure. The destructive process therefore becomes nonlinear.

Early degradation is subtle.

Terminal collapse is abrupt.

This is why a Rzez-damaged bridge may carry traffic safely for months and then fail under a lighter load than it endured the previous day. The relevant question is not how much strength remains in the abstract. It is whether the remaining paths happen to survive the next redistribution of stress.

### Entropic bias

The Rzez does not command matter to disintegrate by brute force. Such a process would require immense energy and produce heat, radiation, shock, or some other obvious transfer.

Instead, the field creates an **entropic bias**: a localized condition in which disordered configurations become easier for the target to enter than ordered ones.

The target supplies most of the destructive energy itself.

Gravity pulls weakened stone apart. Residual stress shatters glass. Internal pressure ruptures tissue. Thermal cycling separates metal grains. Ocean pressure penetrates a failing crust. Tectonic stress, accumulated over millions of years, becomes available all at once when the structures restraining it lose continuity.

The Rzez does not overpower structure. It removes structure’s privilege of remaining stable.

### Structural characterization

Before the field can propagate efficiently, the instrument must characterize the target.

This process is often misunderstood as scanning. It is more accurately described as a temporary resonance between the instrument and the structural pattern of the target. Homogeneous materials can be characterized almost instantly. Complex organisms, composite structures, cities, and planets require longer observation or multiple synchronized emitters.

Characterization identifies:

- repeating material domains,
- paths along which stress is distributed,
- interfaces between unlike materials,
- existing defects,
- active repair mechanisms,
- and the smallest number of relationships whose failure will force wider collapse.

A skilled operator does not “increase power” indiscriminately. The operator chooses which continuities the target cannot afford to lose.

### Propagation

The field propagates most efficiently through materials that are crystalline, homogeneous, mechanically stressed, continuous over long distances, or organized around repeating patterns.

Propagation is slower through porous composites, amorphous solids, actively changing substances, turbulent fluids, highly heterogeneous structures, and systems capable of rapid self-repair.

This distinction made the Rzez useful in mining. A field could follow a mineral vein while ignoring surrounding stone. It also made the weapon devastating against civilizations whose infrastructure formed large, regular, interconnected systems.

A city with standardized steel, continuous foundations, shared water mains, and deep transport tunnels is easier to characterize than a settlement built from irregular stone, timber, earth, and independently supported structures.

The same efficiency that makes civilization possible can make civilization legible to the Rzez.

### Scale

Rzez effects do not scale merely by increasing output. Each scale requires a different engineering approach.

A hand instrument characterizes a local target.

A quarry array establishes boundaries around a material domain.

A siege engine exploits load-bearing continuity across a structure.

A strategic array uses infrastructure as a transmission network.

A planetary Rzez synchronizes geological systems until the world itself becomes the propagation medium.

The planetary implementation was therefore not simply an enormous blade or emitter. It was an architecture of carefully placed weaknesses.

### The Quiet

Many major Rzez events include a brief interval immediately before catastrophic failure during which sound, vibration, and visible motion appear suppressed. This phenomenon is called **the Quiet**.

Witnesses describe wind ceasing without atmospheric cause, machinery no longer rattling, footsteps losing their echo, voices sounding remote, and a bodily sensation that pressure has increased despite no measurable change.

The Quiet may last less than a second or several minutes. It does not precede every collapse, but where it occurs, terminal failure usually follows.

The accepted engineering explanation is that vibration becomes increasingly localized as structural paths cease transmitting energy. A less accepted model argues that the field temporarily traps mechanical information in disconnected domains.

Religious traditions claim the Quiet is the moment in which matter realizes it has been abandoned.

Engineers publicly reject that language.

Many engineers still refuse to speak during the Quiet.

## Physical forms and engineering lineage

### The hand instrument

The best-known form resembles a narrow, dark blade between sixty and ninety centimeters long. The apparent edge is not sharp. Several preserved descriptions emphasize that it could not cut cloth when inactive.

The active components lie within the spine:

- a sequence of nested resonant cavities,
- a focusing core grown from a highly ordered crystalline seed,
- sacrificial discontinuity layers intended to prevent feedback,
- and a grip assembly that isolates the operator’s bones and connective tissue from the field.

When inactive, the instrument appears visually unremarkable. When active, observers report a faint loss of contrast around the edge, delayed reflections, surface details that seem misregistered, pressure in the teeth, and a sound compared to distant cloth tearing beneath deep water.

The weapon need not penetrate its target. Sustained contact is sufficient.

The blade form also carries symbolic weight. Soldiers understood swords. They did not understand cohesion graphs. The shape concealed an engineering revolution inside the visual language of ordinary violence.

### Quarry frames

The first industrial systems consisted of several emitters placed around a target mass. Each weakened a selected material network while the others suppressed propagation across the intended boundary.

Quarry frames were used for ore extraction, tunnel construction, controlled demolition, recovery of fragile artifacts, recycling of mixed materials, and separation of fused components.

Contemporary advertisements praised them as “bloodless wedges” and “the end of blasting.” Quarry records document dramatic reductions in worker deaths.

The same records also contain the first unexplained disappearances of support columns, machinery beds, and entire cavern floors.

### Medical separators

Medical Rzez instruments were small, heavily damped, and designed to operate for fractions of a second. They were tested for separating calcified growths, releasing scar tissue, removing tumors, clearing fused joints, and debriding necrotic material.

Their promise was extraordinary. A surgeon could theoretically remove pathological tissue without cutting healthy structures.

The program ended after several patients developed delayed failures months later. In the most notorious case, a woman treated for a calcified heart valve recovered completely, then died when the repaired valve, adjacent connective tissue, and part of the vessel wall separated during sleep.

The attending surgeon wrote:

> *The operation succeeded. The patient healed. The field continued.*

### Siege anchors

Siege variants used anchors placed against walls, foundations, gates, piers, tunnels, or defensive earthworks.

They rarely caused immediate destruction. Their purpose was to introduce invisible structural uncertainty. A fortress might survive inspection and bombardment, then fail days later when a gate moved, a cistern filled, or defenders shifted artillery.

Because the effect could be delayed, even an unsuccessful infiltration created fear. Every crack became evidence. Every creak became a warning. Commanders evacuated defensible positions because they could no longer trust the distinction between intact and doomed.

### Strategic lattices

Strategic Rzez systems linked several weak emitters across an urban or regional network. They exploited continuous infrastructure such as rails, aqueducts, power conduits, mines, and foundation grids.

A lattice did not need to destroy every building. It needed to make the city’s systems invalidate one another. A water main weakened a street. The street shifted and loaded a tunnel. The tunnel fractured a foundation. The foundation collapse severed energy conduits. Repairs attached new material to contaminated old material and inherited the failure pattern.

Strategic lattices made reconstruction part of the attack.

### Planetary arrays

A planetary Rzez was a distributed system placed at geologically significant points across a world: fault intersections, deep mines, mantle access shafts, ocean trenches, conductive mineral formations, and major infrastructure nexuses.

The emitters synchronized through the target itself.

The planet became transmission medium, resonant chamber, and eventual executioner.

The array did not attack every atom. It attacked the relationships required for the world to remain a world:

- crustal load transfer,
- mantle convection continuity,
- mineral phase stability,
- ocean-basin containment,
- deep structural coupling,
- and the persistence of large-scale aggregation.

Once these systems became mutually unstable, gravity completed the process.

## Discovery traditions

No single account of the Rzez’s discovery is universally accepted. The historical engineer’s account is best supported, but older myths contain details later associated with actual Rzez behavior.

### The Gentle Opening

**Evidentiary grade: A**

The technical account credits Varek Ilyr, a quarry engineer employed by the First Quarry Houses (a federation of mining guilds that dominated early Meridian mineral extraction).

The Houses worked deep deposits in which valuable crystal seams were embedded inside unstable host rock. Explosives shattered the ore. Heat contaminated it. Manual extraction killed workers.

Ilyr proposed weakening cohesion along the crystal boundary rather than increasing force.

The first controlled trial extracted a thirty-seven-meter seam intact while leaving the surrounding cavern standing. The event became known as the **Gentle Opening**.

A surviving payroll tablet records bonuses paid to forty-eight workers. A later memorial lists the names of seven others who died when a neighboring support formation failed two weeks afterward. The official history omitted those names.

### Saint Rezha, the famine butcher

**Evidentiary grade: E**

In eastern tradition, the Rzez is attributed to Saint Rezha, a butcher during the Ash Famine (a legendary seven-year crop failure remembered across the eastern provinces).

Rezha prayed for the ability to divide the few remaining animals among every hungry household. Her prayer was answered literally. Whatever she touched separated perfectly.

At first she divided meat.

Then bone.

Then wooden tables.

Then the stones of her home.

Unable to embrace her children without harming them, she wrapped her hands in chains and walked into the mountains. The mountain opened beneath her feet.

The oldest surviving hymn ends:

> *She asked for enough pieces to save everyone. The god did not understand that some things must remain whole.*

The myth predates Ilyr by at least three centuries in its earliest datable form. Skeptics note that later copies may have been revised after the industrial invention. Believers answer that the oldest fragment already describes delayed collapse and the inability to touch loved ones, two details absent from early public engineering accounts.

### The stone that remembered sand

**Evidentiary grade: C for the artifact tradition; E for the supernatural claim**

A southern mining tradition claims that the Rzez was not invented but rediscovered.

Miners beneath Keth Aru (an ancient salt and obsidian city later abandoned after a subsidence disaster) uncovered a black stone that whispered the former names of everything around it.

When placed against granite, it reminded granite that it had once been sediment.

When placed against metal, it reminded metal that it had once been ore.

When placed against bone, it reminded bone that it had once been dust.

The object appears in three independent temple inventories under different names. One inventory describes it as “a smooth black wedge that may not be stored upon joined stone.” Another records the collapse of a reliquary floor.

Modern identity theorists treat the story as a primitive account of structural memory. The Compact Commission classifies the artifact as unverified and prohibits excavation at Keth Aru.

### The Severed God

**Evidentiary grade: E**

An outlawed theological tradition claims that material reality was created from the body of a murdered god. Matter remains coherent because every fragment remembers that it once belonged to the same divine whole.

The Rzez destroys that memory.

Under this interpretation, the weapon does not increase entropy. It interrupts Creation’s continuing act of self-recognition.

The doctrine was prohibited by the Concordant Temples because it implied that divine unity could be technologically attacked. Possession of the full text was once punishable by death.

Fragments recovered after Valec include a line absent from earlier copies:

> *The first butcher did not kill the god. The first butcher taught the pieces to forget one another.*

Whether the line predates the planetary attack is disputed.

### The Last Wedge of Orsa

**Evidentiary grade: D**

The mountain clans of Orsa (a highland region known for dry-stone fortifications and resistance to imperial rule) tell of a black wedge used to end a siege without battle.

A clan mother placed the wedge in the road beneath an invading army. The road became gravel only after the last supply wagon entered the pass. The cliffs then descended “without falling,” sealing the army beneath layers of loose stone.

Imperial records confirm that the Ninth Expedition vanished in the Orsan Pass. They blame an earthquake. No seismic disturbance appears in surviving observatory logs.

The Orsans insist the wedge was buried with the clan mother and that the grave has never remained in the same place for two winters.

## Historical uses, alleged uses, and surviving stories

### The First Quarry accidents

**Approximate date:** Early Meridian Age  
**Targets:** Mineral seams, quarry walls, support formations  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

The earliest Rzez incidents were not military. They occurred in mines.

The First Quarry Houses maintained meticulous output records but poor casualty records. Modern reconstruction identifies at least twenty-nine collapses with features consistent with uncontrolled cohesion propagation.

One report describes a tunnel wall that “shed its face like flour” while leaving carved inscriptions suspended for several seconds before they, too, fell apart. Another records a support pillar that passed every load test and then separated into fist-sized crystals after a worker struck it with a wooden cup.

The Quarry Houses blamed vibration, poor workmanship, sabotage, and worker superstition. Privately, they created the first isolation protocols.

A recovered memorandum states:

> *Do not permit the separated stone to touch the supports. Do not reuse the separated stone. Do not build sleeping quarters beneath any chamber where the field has been established.*

The document is dated forty-six years before the Houses publicly admitted that propagation beyond a target boundary was possible.

### Deren Keep

**Approximate date:** 81 years after the Gentle Opening  
**Target:** Deren Keep (a frontier fortress controlling the western grain road)  
**Operator:** The Crown Army of Merovar (an early expansionist kingdom)  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

Deren Keep is the first documented military use.

During negotiations, a military engineer disguised a quarry emitter as a survey instrument and attached it to the outer wall for fewer than four minutes.

Nothing happened.

The defenders mocked the retreating army.

Six days later, the keep opened its main gate to receive provisions. The change in load distribution caused the western curtain wall to collapse inward. Barracks, granaries, and civilian shelters had been built against it.

The Crown Army entered without bombardment.

Military correspondence praised the victory as “bloodless engineering.” Casualty ledgers record more than two thousand dead, most of them noncombatants crushed beneath structures that appeared undamaged minutes earlier.

Varek Ilyr’s attributed response contains one sentence:

> *You have mistaken silence for mercy.*

Some historians argue the line was written decades later and placed in Ilyr’s mouth by antiwar scholars. It nevertheless became the most famous sentence associated with him.

### The Ossuary of Kareth

**Approximate date:** Middle Meridian Age  
**Target:** The Ossuary of Kareth (a vast underground burial complex sacred to several rival dynasties)  
**Alleged operator:** Unknown  
**Evidentiary grade:** B for the destruction; D for intentional Rzez use

The Ossuary contained the preserved bones of nearly one million dead and served as both religious center and dynastic archive.

During the War of Seven Heirs, the entire complex began to fail from its deepest chambers outward. Stone shelves became sand. Sealed sarcophagi opened along their seams. Bones softened, bent, and separated into mineral powder and fibrous residue.

No emitter was found.

Every faction denied responsibility.

The event ended the war because none of the heirs could prove ancestry after the physical genealogical archive was destroyed. This consequence produced a persistent rumor that the true target was not the tomb but the legal continuity of the dynasties.

A surviving priest wrote:

> *They did not kill our fathers. They killed the fact that they had been our fathers.*

Later theorists cite Kareth as possible evidence that the Rzez may act upon institutional identity when that identity is materially encoded. Mainstream scholarship considers the claim metaphorical.

### The Glass Fleet of Serevan

**Approximate date:** Middle Meridian Age  
**Target:** The Glass Fleet (the ceremonial and military navy of Serevan, a maritime city-state famous for ceramic-composite ships)  
**Alleged operator:** The Veyran League (a rival coalition of port cities)  
**Evidentiary grade:** B

The Glass Fleet was thought resistant to Rzez attack because its hulls used layered ceramic, resin, wood, and metal rather than continuous steel.

The fleet sailed from harbor during a midsummer review. Witnesses described no explosion or attack. Shortly after the ships entered deep water, their hulls began separating by material.

Metal fasteners fell free.

Ceramic armor peeled away in tiles.

Wooden ribs split into fibers.

Resin seams became liquid.

The vessels did not sink immediately. They unfolded.

Serevan accused the Veyran League of contaminating repair materials over several years. The League denied involvement and argued that the fleet’s standardized construction created a hidden continuity across otherwise heterogeneous materials.

Divers later found several hull sections intact but incapable of being reassembled. New fasteners loosened. Adhesives failed to cure at the contact surface. The harbor authority sealed the wreck field.

Sailors still claim that on windless nights the water above the wrecks becomes perfectly flat and produces the sound of ropes being slowly untwisted.

### The Seven Bridges of Orel

**Approximate date:** Late Meridian Age  
**Target:** Seven bridges across the River Orel (a major transport corridor serving the city of the same name)  
**Operator:** Confirmed Rzez sabotage cell  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

Seven bridges were exposed to nearly identical siege charges.

They did not fail together.

One collapsed within minutes. Two failed within three days. Three were dismantled preemptively. The seventh remained in service for eleven years after repeated inspection.

It finally disintegrated beneath a single vegetable cart carrying less than one-quarter of its rated load.

The event established one of the central principles of Rzez forensics: exposure determines a field of possible failures, not a reliable countdown.

The bridge’s final inspector survived because he was standing on the riverbank. He later testified:

> *It did not break. Every piece selected its own direction.*

The Seven Bridges led to the doctrine of **suspected unmaking**, under which any structure that could not be proven untouched was treated as lost.

Enemy agents quickly learned that forged contamination notices could empty cities more cheaply than actual deployment.

### The Night of Hollow Armor

**Approximate date:** Forty-two years before the Sixth Compact  
**Target:** The royal guard of Avel Taran (a militarized plateau kingdom)  
**Alleged operator:** Palace faction or foreign intelligence service  
**Evidentiary grade:** C

During the coronation of Queen Sera Taran, two hundred guards assembled in ceremonial plate armor.

At the first sounding of the coronation bells, the armor began shedding thin metallic layers. Breastplates opened along grain boundaries. Helmets collapsed around their wearers. Sword blades exfoliated into scales.

Most guards survived because the armor failed outward. Seventeen died when structural rings at the neck and joints tightened before separating.

The event produced the phrase “hollow armor,” meaning an institution that preserves appearance after losing function.

No field generator was recovered. Investigators concluded that the royal armory itself had been contaminated, possibly years earlier.

A popular rumor claims the attack was not intended to kill the guard but to reveal that the queen’s authority was structurally empty. The queen ruled for thirty-one years and banned all metaphors involving metal.

### The White Orchard

**Approximate date:** Thirty-six years before the Sixth Compact  
**Target:** An experimental agricultural district near Leth Var (a university city specializing in biological engineering)  
**Cause:** Industrial release, officially accidental  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

A low-intensity field escaped from a recycling facility and passed through an orchard.

The trees appeared healthy for an entire season.

They flowered normally.

Their fruit ripened.

At harvest, every major branch detached at the point where it joined the trunk. Over the next week, trunks separated into fibrous bundles. Leaves remained green for days after losing structural connection to the trees.

Seeds from the fruit produced healthy offspring.

The incident remains central to debates over whether the Rzez damages matter, present organization, inherited pattern, or only the continuity of an existing object.

A groundskeeper reported that one child could still identify which fallen branch belonged to which tree, even after all branches had been mixed. When asked how, the child said, “They are still trying to remember where they were.”

The statement appears in the official record because the investigating magistrate considered it evidence of trauma. Later identity theorists treat it as something else.

### The Bell Chamber experiments

**Approximate date:** Thirty years before the Sixth Compact  
**Target:** Controlled alloy samples  
**Institution:** The Institute of Applied Continuity at Kharos (the leading pre-Compact research academy)  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

Researchers suspended bells made from different metals and exposed them to equal field intensities. Each bell was struck hourly.

Long before visible degradation appeared, stable tones separated into clusters of slightly displaced harmonics. The bells no longer produced a single acoustic identity.

This led to the development of cohesion sonography, still used to detect contamination.

The final bell continued vibrating for nine hours after it had visibly collapsed into flakes.

The experiment log records that the sound moved around the chamber even though no intact resonant body remained.

No accepted theory explains the observation.

A later custodian claimed that the bell resumed sounding each year on the anniversary of Valec. The Institute dismissed him. The chamber was sealed anyway.

### The Marrowhouse Study

**Approximate date:** Twenty-seven years before the Sixth Compact  
**Target:** Living organisms under controlled exposure  
**Institution:** Marrowhouse Clinical Laboratory (a military medical facility later dissolved)  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

Twelve condemned animals were exposed to increasing field intensity.

The lead investigator wrote:

> *No subject displayed the expected signs of burning, poisoning, or ordinary necrosis. The animals instead became structurally uncertain.*

The phrase entered medical literature.

The study was terminated when one subject remained alive for nineteen hours despite losing functional skeletal continuity. The surviving notes describe the animal attempting to stand after its bones could no longer transmit load.

All copies were ordered destroyed. At least four survive.

The name “Marrowhouse” later became a curse among soldiers exposed to suspected Rzez effects.

### The assassination of Engineer-Magistrate Olan Vey

**Approximate date:** Seventeen years before the Sixth Compact  
**Target:** Olan Vey (the jurist-engineer who drafted early Rzez controls)  
**Alleged operator:** Unknown  
**Evidentiary grade:** B

Vey died while delivering testimony before the Kharosi Assembly.

The stone lectern separated into layers beneath his hands. His chair collapsed. The floor remained intact.

An autopsy found no burns, poison, or conventional injury sufficient to explain death. Connective tissues around the heart had lost organization.

The attack demonstrated an alarming degree of selectivity. The field appears to have been tuned to Vey, the lectern, and the chair while sparing adjacent structures.

His final recorded words were:

> *You cannot regulate a principle by pretending it is an object.*

The murder accelerated passage of the first comprehensive prohibition.

### The destruction of Valec

**Approximate date:** Year Zero of the Compact Calendar  
**Target:** Valec (an industrial world with 6.2 billion inhabitants and extensive deep-crust infrastructure)  
**Operator:** Officially unresolved; several states possessed partial capability  
**Evidentiary grade:** A

Valec is the only universally accepted planetary Rzez deployment.

The planet’s prosperity depended upon regularity. Its cities shared standardized materials. Its continents were linked by deep transport, energy, and water systems. Its mantle contained unusually continuous crystalline formations.

These features made Valec efficient.

They also made it legible.

#### Initial anomalies

The first signs appeared ordinary:

- unexplained pipe failures,
- widespread cracking in polished stone,
- bridge cables shedding metallic dust,
- increased sediment in reservoirs,
- machine tools losing calibration,
- and a rise in spontaneous bone injuries.

Officials attributed the events to manufacturing defects, seismic stress, and public panic.

Discussion of entropic attack was suppressed.

#### The Gray Season

Over four months, dust accumulated across major cities despite clear weather.

Buildings faded because their surfaces continuously shed microscopic grains.

Roads softened.

Metal tools lost edges.

Mountain snowpacks darkened with mineral powder.

People began calling the period the Gray Season.

The government continued to deny attack because no emitter network had been found. Later investigators concluded that many emitters had been disguised as geological monitoring stations installed over decades.

#### Continental descent

The first continental collapse occurred without a major earthquake.

The Aster Plateau descended nearly seven hundred meters over nine hours. The rock beneath it had not melted. It had lost the capacity to transmit load across geological distance.

Oceans entered the fractured crust.

Steam eruptions followed.

The planetary array propagated into deep mantle structures.

#### Final phase

Valec did not explode.

Its crust became granular and migrated under gravity. Continents spread into ocean basins. Mountain ranges slumped. The mantle separated into unstable convection domains. The magnetic field fragmented.

The atmosphere filled with mineral aerosols.

Orbital observers watched recognizable geography disappear over eleven days.

The final verified transmission from the surface came from the Halden Geological Observatory:

> *The instruments say the mountain is still here. The window says it is moving. I no longer know which one is lying.*

Evacuation craft failed in large numbers because their materials had already been exposed. Some reached orbit and came apart during docking. Others survived for months before structural systems began to inherit the pattern from contaminated cargo or passengers’ possessions.

Fewer than three million people escaped.

The event created the Sixth Compact of Kharos (the multinational treaty that prohibited Rzez engineering above tightly controlled industrial thresholds).

#### The Valec soil surveys

Twenty-three years later, investigators found that surviving fragments contained chemically ordinary matter.

The elements remained.

The isotopic ratios remained.

Much of the mineral composition remained.

What did not remain were large, persistent structures.

Metal could be smelted but not forged reliably. Stone could be compressed but not made load-bearing. Biological material could grow only as thin films. Crystals stopped developing beyond characteristic sizes.

The commission concluded:

> *Valec was not reduced to debris. It was reduced to matter incapable of becoming a world again.*

### The broken moon of Issa

**Approximate date:** Unknown; possibly pre-Meridian  
**Target:** Issa’s inner moon (a small natural satellite orbiting the gas giant Issa)  
**Alleged operator:** Unknown or nonhuman  
**Evidentiary grade:** C for anomalous structure; E for Rzez attribution

The inner moon of Issa is not fragmented in the ordinary sense. It remains roughly spherical, but radar reveals that its interior consists of billions of unattached stone domains separated by microscopic gaps.

The object should have compacted under gravity. It has not.

No conventional impact explains the lack of melt, shock, or mixed strata.

Ancient navigational poems refer to “the moon that was butchered but forbidden to fall apart.”

Some scholars argue that Issa proves planetary or lunar Rzez technology predates every known civilization. Others propose unusual electrostatic or gravitational conditions.

Compact survey vessels are prohibited from landing.

Three unauthorized expeditions disappeared. The fourth returned with all hull seams loosened and no memory of touching the surface.

### The lost city of Meren

**Approximate date:** Two centuries after the Compact  
**Target:** Meren (a desert city built around a buried pre-Compact research complex)  
**Alleged operator:** Smugglers, cultists, or a surviving autonomous device  
**Evidentiary grade:** D

Meren vanished over the course of one night without explosion.

At dawn, travelers found a shallow basin filled with loose sand, metal flakes, cloth fibers, teeth, glass powder, and unbroken sealed jars. No intact walls or bodies remained.

The jars contained water and were standing upright.

This detail has never been explained.

The official investigation blamed subsidence and mass panic. The report remains classified.

Local nomads refuse to cross the basin. They claim shadows there separate from the people who cast them and continue walking after sunset.

### The Orchard Ship rumor

**Approximate date:** Current era  
**Target:** Unknown  
**Alleged operator:** Valeci survivor network  
**Evidentiary grade:** E

A persistent rumor claims that a hidden Valeci colony travels aboard a vast biological vessel called the Orchard Ship.

The ship is said to be grown from constantly renewing tissue, making it resistant to Rzez characterization. Its inhabitants allegedly preserve the final uncorrupted samples of Valec’s ecosystems.

A darker version claims the ship carries a controlled planetary Rzez and searches for the state responsible for Valec.

No credible sighting exists.

Several governments nevertheless maintain classified procedures for identifying large regenerative vessels.

## Material response

### Ferrous metals

Steel initially shows little visible change.

The earliest measurable effect is acoustic inconsistency. When struck, exposed steel produces several slightly displaced tones instead of one stable resonance.

Later stages include grain-boundary separation, delamination, loss of elasticity, sudden brittle failure, and exfoliation into metallic scales.

Static strength may remain acceptable while cyclic strength collapses. Swords, axles, bridges, turbines, and ship frames are therefore especially dangerous after low-level exposure.

A common field test involves ringing the material three times. If the third tone does not agree with the first two, the object is abandoned.

### Glass and ceramics

Glass is highly vulnerable because of stored internal stress.

Early exposure produces clouding, optical doubling, and fine lines visible only in peripheral vision. Terminal failure is often instantaneous.

Witnesses frequently report that exposed windows remain intact until a room becomes quiet, then collapse together.

Ceramics tend to powder rather than shatter. Layered ceramics may resist propagation but can fail catastrophically at interfaces.

### Stone

Granite and other crystalline stone separate along grain boundaries.

Typical progression includes loss of polish, darkening at mineral inclusions, granular shedding, local subsidence, and conversion to coarse aggregate.

Sedimentary stone delaminates along strata.

Metamorphic stone may fail violently because stored geological stress is released unevenly.

Old buildings can be more dangerous than new ones because centuries of stress, repair, water intrusion, and thermal cycling provide the field with many existing pathways.

### Wood

Wood resists immediate collapse because of its hierarchical, fibrous organization.

The field first attacks bonds between structural levels rather than fibers themselves.

Effects include longitudinal splitting, separation into hairlike bundles, loss of torsional strength, and an odor compared to rain on dead bark.

Living trees may compensate temporarily through resin, growth, and tissue repair. Dead timber cannot.

### Water and fluids

Pure fluids are poor propagation media because they lack persistent structural architecture.

The Rzez rarely destroys an ocean directly.

It destroys the basin, pressure boundaries, dissolved structures, suspended minerals, ice, vessels, and biological systems within the water.

Some accounts claim that exposed water “forgets how to freeze.” Laboratory evidence is inconsistent.

### Bone

Bone exhibits a two-stage response.

The mineral matrix weakens first, producing a chalklike texture. Collagen then loses alignment, making the bone temporarily flexible before failure.

This flexibility is diagnostically important.

During several wars, physicians misclassified exposed patients as suffering from rare metabolic disease because their bones bent rather than fractured.

### Living tissue

Living systems resist low-level exposure through constant repair, but this resistance is finite.

The field preferentially affects structures dependent upon precise organization:

- basement membranes,
- connective tissue,
- vascular walls,
- myelin sheaths,
- extracellular matrices,
- cellular scaffolding,
- and the mechanical junctions between tissues.

Early symptoms include numbness, bruising without trauma, loss of fine motor control, visual distortion, dental pain, fatigue, and the sensation that joints are “slightly misplaced.”

Late exposure is systemic. The body does not decompose conventionally. It loses the relationships required to continue functioning as one body.

Several religions deny ordinary funerary rites to confirmed victims because they dispute whether the deceased remains a single person after terminal structural separation. Valeci communities consider this doctrine an obscenity.

## Operational doctrine

### Contact and establishment

Early instruments required direct contact. Later systems used anchors, prepared surfaces, conductive inclusions, or resonant pathways.

Contact does not merely transfer energy. It establishes a coherent relationship between the field and target.

### Characterization

The instrument maps the target’s cohesion architecture.

For a blade or stone block, characterization may be immediate.

For a fortress, it may require minutes or repeated placements.

For a city, it may require infiltrated sensors and years of material data.

For a planet, it may require geological observation across generations.

### Biasing

The field lowers the stability of selected structural relationships.

The target may still appear unharmed.

At this stage, removal of the instrument does not necessarily reverse the effect.

### Redistribution

Ordinary forces shift toward surviving paths.

This increased burden accelerates failure.

Repairs may worsen the process by attaching fresh material to contaminated structures.

### Cascade

Once key relationships fail, collapse becomes self-sustaining.

Destroying the original emitter may no longer matter.

The target has become capable of continuing its own disassembly.

### Quiet state

Immediately before terminal failure, vibration may cease to transmit normally.

This produces the Quiet.

### Residual pattern

Some targets retain a tendency to impose similar failure upon newly attached material.

This is called **target inheritance** or, less formally, **the butcher’s stain**.

Target inheritance is one of the least understood and most feared Rzez properties.

## Detection and forensic evidence

Early Rzez exposure is difficult to prove.

Investigators rely on inconsistency rather than visible damage.

Methods include:

- acoustic divergence testing,
- microfracture imaging,
- stress-distribution tomography,
- entropic residue analysis,
- repeated load-response comparison,
- crystal-growth testing,
- and sacrificial attachment trials.

The most reliable field principle states:

> *A thing touched by the Rzez cannot repeat itself.*

Identical tests performed minutes apart may produce different results because the remaining structural paths continue to change.

Forensic investigators also look for mismatched failure: objects that retain mass and chemical composition but lose the ability to function as unified structures.

A Rzez crime scene often looks too clean. There is no burn pattern, explosive residue, impact cone, or clear point of mechanical overload.

Instead, everything has simply stopped agreeing with everything else.

## Countermeasures

### Discontinuous construction

Structures can be divided into independent cells so that failure cannot propagate across the whole.

This principle transformed post-Compact architecture. Bridges became segmented. Towers stood on separate foundations. Water systems used deliberate gaps and sacrificial joints.

### Heterogeneous materials

Irregular composites interrupt characterization.

The best defensive materials combine layers with different internal organizations, provided no repeating interface becomes a new propagation path.

### Amorphous alloys

Materials without regular crystal structure reduce field efficiency.

They are expensive, difficult to manufacture, and often mechanically inferior in conventional ways.

### Continuous vibration

Controlled vibration prevents the field from establishing a stable map.

The defense consumes substantial energy and can cause illness, fatigue, and structural wear of its own.

Some military bunkers hum continuously for decades.

### Active reconfiguration

Structures can rotate load among separate frameworks or rearrange internal components so that no cohesion pattern remains stable long enough to characterize.

This approach is common in high-security vessels and rumored to be used by the Orchard Ship.

### Self-repair

Biological or mechanical systems can rebuild damaged relationships faster than low-level fields remove them.

Self-repair does not provide immunity. It turns exposure into a race.

### Isolation doctrine

Once contamination is suspected, affected systems are disconnected from all continuous structures.

Bridges are cut.

Pipes are drained.

Tunnels are collapsed.

Power lines are severed.

Buildings are abandoned.

The defense can resemble the attack.

### False-alarm exploitation

Because isolation is destructive, adversaries often fabricate Rzez evidence.

The Sixth Compact therefore requires independent confirmation before regional disconnection, except where delay would create planetary risk.

The argument over when to cut and when to investigate remains one of the central political dilemmas of the post-Valec world.

## Failure modes of the instrument

### Recursive collapse

The field may enter its own focusing architecture.

The instrument disassembles itself and may propagate into the operator or supporting structure.

### Reflection

Highly ordered or specially tuned materials may return the field toward its source.

Evidence is inconsistent. Some engineers believe “reflection” is a misdiagnosed calibration failure.

### False termination

The instrument may report successful reduction while significant structural paths remain.

The result is incomplete destruction and unpredictable delayed failure.

### Target inheritance

New material attached to a damaged target may acquire the same instability.

This effect makes repair dangerous.

### Operator contamination

Frequent users show elevated rates of connective-tissue failure, bone weakness, sensory distortion, dissociation, and continuity dread.

Whether these effects arise from field leakage, occupational trauma, or both remains unsettled.

### Self-reference

A theoretical Rzez capable of fully characterizing itself may identify its own structure as the most efficient propagation path.

No verified self-characterizing instrument exists.

Several laboratories that attempted one no longer exist either.

## Lesser-known and disputed theoretical properties

### The Rzez may attack information rather than matter

The dominant theory holds that the field alters physical relationships.

A competing theory argues that it corrupts the information specifying which material relationships belong to one object.

This would explain target inheritance, the persistence of Valec’s anti-aggregation effect, and cases in which chemically normal material refuses to form durable structures.

Under this model, the Rzez does not weaken bonds directly. It alters the rule by which the system recognizes those bonds as part of the same continuing thing.

### Cohesion may be partly historical

Several experiments suggest that older structures respond differently from newly assembled copies made of identical material.

This implies that cohesion includes a temporal component: the history of having remained together.

If true, ancient walls, heirlooms, living organisms, and longstanding institutions may possess forms of continuity not reducible to current material arrangement.

It also implies that restoration may fail because a repaired object lacks the uninterrupted history that once made it whole.

### The field may propagate through identity

A controversial model proposes that the Rzez can follow identity relationships rather than physical contact.

Supporters cite:

- replacement components inheriting damage,
- the simultaneous failure of objects made from the same original ingot,
- synchronized fractures among ceremonial artifacts symbolically treated as one set,
- and the Ossuary of Kareth, where physical destruction erased dynastic continuity.

Critics argue that hidden material commonalities explain these cases.

No sanctioned experiment may test symbolic propagation.

### Observation may stabilize a target

Some records suggest that continuously measured structures resist low-level propagation longer than unobserved ones.

The effect may result from active testing, vibration, or repair rather than observation itself.

A minority interpretation claims that a structure more firmly represented in external records possesses additional informational continuity.

Valeci memorial practice reflects this idea. Families maintain exhaustive records of lost people and objects, saying, “What is remembered remains difficult to butcher.”

### The Rzez may have a minimum meaningful target

At microscopic scales, the field becomes unreliable.

Some theorists argue that an isolated molecule has too little structural identity to butcher. The Rzez requires a target that is meaningfully one thing.

This raises an unresolved question: who or what defines meaningful unity?

The instrument?

The operator?

The target?

An observer?

The culture that named the object?

### The Quiet may be trapped mechanical information

The standard theory treats the Quiet as failed vibration transmission.

A more radical model proposes that sound and motion continue locally but cannot cross newly severed structural domains. The energy remains present as inaccessible internal states.

This may explain the Bell Chamber, where sound apparently persisted after the bell had collapsed.

If correct, sufficiently large Rzez events could trap enormous amounts of mechanical information and release it unpredictably if continuity were restored.

### Rzez damage may be reversible before recognition

Several fragments describe restoration attempts that succeeded only when performed before the target “understood” its damaged state.

Engineers translate this as repair before cascade threshold.

Mystical schools interpret it literally: a structure may continue to be whole while no observer has registered its failure.

No ethical experiment can distinguish these interpretations.

### It may act upon boundaries more readily than substances

The field consistently affects interfaces: grain boundaries, joints, membranes, shorelines, fault planes, social and legal transitions.

Some theorists therefore argue that the true target of the Rzez is not matter but boundaries.

A weapon tuned to boundaries could theoretically separate:

- a city from its supply network,
- a memory from the person who formed it,
- a name from the thing it denotes,
- a law from the authority enforcing it,
- or a world from the space that contains it.

No verified device has demonstrated these effects.

Several myths claim otherwise.

### Institutional Rzez

The phrase **institutional Rzez** originally described the social consequences of suspected contamination: distrust, evacuation, broken supply chains, and governments turning against their own engineers.

A forbidden school treats the phrase literally.

Its adherents claim institutions possess cohesion architectures composed of records, roles, expectations, rituals, and shared memory. They argue that a sufficiently abstract Rzez could invalidate those relationships without physically harming participants.

The sudden collapse of three pre-Compact states is cited as evidence.

Mainstream historians identify ordinary causes: war, famine, succession disputes, and panic.

The forbidden school answers that these are precisely the forces a Rzez would use.

### Celestial propagation

The broken moon of Issa suggests that low-level anti-aggregation may persist under gravity for geological periods.

If a Rzez pattern can inhabit a celestial body, it might spread through impacts, ejecta, or shared orbital material.

This possibility underlies the Compact’s absolute prohibition on importing Valec fragments.

Several black markets nevertheless trade alleged Valec dust as a weapon, relic, or charm against attachment.

Most samples are fraudulent.

Not all have been tested.

### The weapon may not distinguish destruction from liberation

The earliest quarry texts describe the field as releasing material from unwanted attachment.

This language survives in cults that worship the Rzez as an instrument of liberation.

They claim all suffering arises from forced continuity: bodies bound to pain, people bound to history, souls bound to matter, worlds bound to form.

To them, Valec was not murdered. It was freed from the burden of remaining one thing.

These cults are small, secretive, and disproportionately represented among attempts to recover prohibited equations.

## Continuity dread

Continuity dread is the persistent fear that objects, bodies, institutions, or relationships only appear intact.

Affected individuals may compulsively inspect walls, bridges, tools, bones, skin, legal documents, and family records. Some repeatedly ask whether a familiar object is “still the same object.”

Severe cases reject sleep because personal identity may not persist across unconsciousness.

The condition first appeared among Orel bridge inspectors and later became common among Valeci survivors.

A survivor wrote:

> *I know the table is whole. I ate from it this morning. But that is what the bridges taught us: use is not proof of wholeness.*

Clinical schools disagree over whether continuity dread is a trauma disorder, a rational response to entropic threat, a neurological effect of low-level exposure, or a culturally mediated combination of all three.

## Law, ethics, and prohibition

The Sixth Compact prohibits more than construction.

It forbids:

- computational modeling of planetary propagation,
- manufacture of cohesion-focusing cores above industrial tolerance,
- possession of complete field equations,
- preservation of emitter synchronization protocols,
- unsupervised study of Valec material,
- and symbolic or identity-based Rzez experiments.

The prohibition remains controversial.

Engineers argue that suppression prevents development of defenses.

Governments answer that every defensive study reconstructs part of the weapon.

Religious authorities support prohibition for different reasons. Some consider the Rzez an offense against life. Others consider it an offense against matter, history, divine unity, or the possibility of repair.

The Compact’s preamble states:

> *Some instruments kill those who live upon a world. The Rzez kills the proposition that a world may hold them.*

A persistent legal problem concerns incomplete knowledge. A researcher may rediscover a prohibited principle without knowing its lineage. Several mathematicians have been prosecuted for equations they claimed were purely abstract.

The Compact courts answer that dangerous truth does not become safe because it was independently found.

## Cultural afterlife

The Rzez transformed language.

To “hear the third tone” means to detect hidden failure.

“Hollow armor” describes an institution that preserves appearance without function.

“The Quiet” refers to the moment before an inevitable collapse becomes visible.

“Valec stone” means something chemically genuine but incapable of bearing trust.

Children in several regions play a game in which they build towers from mismatched objects and remove connections one at a time. The player who causes the collapse is called the Butcher.

Valeci communities reject the game.

Architects leave visible seams in public buildings to show that no structure pretends to impossible unity.

Wedding rituals sometimes include two separate cups rather than one shared vessel, symbolizing chosen connection without loss of independent continuity.

In mourning, Valeci families record not only names but relationships: who knew whom, who repaired what, who stood where, which objects belonged together. Their memorial books are called **cohesion ledgers**.

## Unresolved questions

The following questions remain open:

- Does the Rzez increase entropy, or does it alter access to ordered states?
- Does it damage matter, relationships, information, history, or identity?
- Why does the Quiet occur?
- Can terminal Rzez damage ever be reversed?
- Is Valec still decaying?
- Can Rzez contamination spread through shared origin rather than contact?
- Did prehuman or nonhuman civilizations possess the technology?
- Is the broken moon of Issa an ancient victim?
- Can observation or memory stabilize cohesion?

- Is the Rzez a weapon applied to reality, or an exposed property reality always possessed?

## Engineering interpretation

The Rzez demonstrates the difference between destruction and disassembly.

Destruction generally requires energy sufficient to overwhelm a target.

Disassembly requires knowledge sufficient to identify what the target cannot survive losing.

This is why the technology has always attracted engineers, strategists, reformers, assassins, and tyrants.

It offers a terrible economy.

A small intervention, correctly placed, can defeat a much larger structure.

A blade can defeat a gate.

An anchor can defeat a city.

A lattice can defeat a civilization.

A network can defeat a world.

The deepest warning of the Rzez is therefore not that matter is fragile.

It is that strength is often only the successful coordination of many small continuities, and that anything assembled can be studied as a problem of where to make separation begin.
