# Editorial Voice and Method

## Apparent voice

The Blackglass recension speaks in a restrained technical-historical voice. It
takes engineering consequences seriously, describes horror precisely, and does
not require the reader to accept every metaphysical interpretation.

The voice is institutional without being fully official, learned without being
omniscient, and skeptical without treating folklore as worthless.

## Editorial practices established by the Rzez

### Standalone clarification

People, places, institutions, and eras receive brief parenthetical clarification
when first introduced so an entry remains readable outside its original archival
context.

### Claim-level evidence

Grades A through E attach to incidents or claims. The editors may accept that an
event occurred while rejecting one explanation for it.

### Competing explanations

Technical, legal, religious, cultural, and survivor interpretations can coexist.
The editors state which explanation is dominant and why, while retaining
minority theories that account for unresolved evidence.

### Precision before spectacle

Entries explain mechanism, propagation, material response, detection,
countermeasures, and failure. Catastrophe follows from systems and decisions
rather than from unexplained increases in power.

### Consequence beyond deployment

Language, architecture, law, ritual, medicine, trauma, fraud, and scholarship
are treated as part of a technology's history.

### Quotations as evidence and memory

Attributed fragments, testimony, field records, and sayings appear when they
reveal character, uncertainty, or cultural survival. Their authenticity should
be stated rather than assumed.

## What the voice should avoid

- omniscient declarations unsupported by the frame;
- modern jokes that collapse the archival atmosphere;
- vague claims that a technology is simply beyond understanding;
- treating Grade E as synonymous with false;
- resolving every legend into engineering or every experiment into mysticism;
- repetitive moral condemnation in place of analysis;
- and gratuitous technical terminology without consequences or limits.

## Multiple editorial layers

Future entries may reveal identifiable editorial layers, but their markers must
be consistent. Possible layers include:

- an original technical record;
- an official Compact summary;
- a later Blackglass annotation;
- a translator's note;
- a censor's omission;
- or a recovered primary fragment.

Do not introduce these labels casually. Once named, a layer may become a recurring
voice with its own knowledge, date, loyalties, and vocabulary.
