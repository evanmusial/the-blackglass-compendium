# Open Collection-Frame Questions

## Identity

The Blackglass name's first meaning remains unknown. Material, censorship,
and mirror explanations may have converged before the present recension, but
their sequence is unresolved. The official Codex's continued existence and the
existence of any physically or informationally complete Blackglass recension
are also open questions.

## Compilation

The current filing layer cannot be assigned securely to a compiler. It may have
been created inside the Compact Commission, stolen from it, or reconstructed
independently. The editors may be one group, a succession across generations,
or a later synthesis of several chains. How much material was removed for danger
rather than falsehood remains unsettled. The Prior Hands and the reason their
names disappeared before the earliest surviving witness families diverged
remain unknown.

## Reader

The represented access fixes neither the reader's role nor the legality of
possession. Investigator, scholar, criminal, survivor, official, and an
unspecified person outside the fiction remain possibilities. Its medium is
likewise unresolved within a lineage found as books, distributed archives,
recovered data, carvings, and isolated leaves.

## Completeness and order

The beginning of the surviving sequence does not explain why it begins there.
Missing books may have been destroyed, withheld, untranslated, never recovered,
or never part of the same historical corpus. The `A` prefix in `A-7.01` remains
unexplained, as does whether the present order reproduces the official Codex
or a Blackglass reconstruction.

## Reliability

Deliberately planted official entries, contamination by a described technology,
and manipulation known to later editors remain unresolved possibilities. Even
the evidence that would distinguish a lying frame from one false entry remains
an open question.

## Distribution and response

The proximate-medium pattern remains unexplained. Pages found in open space
may be ordinary debris, planted relics, or objects without a recoverable parent.
Landscape correspondences may be natural analogues, ancient engineering,
category errors, or a form of record not yet understood. Remote dark-mirror
traditions may have spread through lost contact, recurrent encounters, or an
inheritance older than present routes.

Trouble may follow Blackglass, Blackglass may follow trouble, or both may follow
a third cause. The Narrow Proof may have been an empty boast, a removed
philological argument, or a dangerous completion whose absence is itself
containment. None of these possibilities supplies a settled answer.
